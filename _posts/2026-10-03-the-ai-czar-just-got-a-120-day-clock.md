The float is over. Jay Clayton, in his own words to the Wall Street Journal on
Saturday, confirms the White House has created its AI task force — the
"Super Intelligence Force," which takes the president's preferred name for AI —
and that he will chair it, with 120 days to report on the technology's risks
and opportunities and to recommend what role the federal government should
play in overseeing it. A senior White House official says that effectively
makes Clayton, still serving as Director of National Intelligence, Trump's AI
czar. The seat this column has tracked since the "three or four days" clock
was first floated stops being a rumor and becomes a job with a deadline.

## The governance clock now runs to the end of January

**What happened.** The WSJ's exclusive, published Saturday evening, quotes
Clayton: "The president asked that a group be put together that was going to
ensure exactly what he said, which is that we stay the leaders in
superintelligence, and that the interests of the American people are put
first." The new group — the Super Intelligence Force — has 120 days to deliver
a report on AI's risks and opportunities and to recommend the federal
government's role in overseeing it. A senior White House official told the WSJ
that leading the group "effectively makes" Clayton the AI czar, the role David
Sacks vacated in March when his special-government-employee term ran out.

**Why it matters.** This stops being a personnel story and becomes a planning
input. The 120-day clock lands the report in late January 2027, ahead of most
budget cycles, and whoever writes it sets the definitions that govern frontier
capability, open weights, and the buildout. That the czar wears the DNI hat
matters more than the hat itself: the civilian AI portfolio is now fused with
the intelligence posture, which is the containment-and-red-team frame rather
than the industrial-policy one. For an operator, the appointment is the signal
to build on worst-case governance assumptions between now and the report, and
to treat its language as a preview of enforcement.

## What I'm watching

Whether a formal White House announcement follows the WSJ exclusive, and what
the report deadline does to the labs' own timelines. The name itself is the
first policy decision the group has already made: "Super Intelligence Force"
is a mandate about primacy, framed as a national-security problem with a
reporting clock, not an economic one — so watch its language for the same
frame on open weights and export policy.
