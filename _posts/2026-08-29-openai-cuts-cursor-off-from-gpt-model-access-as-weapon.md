OpenAI notified SpaceX it will wind down the contract that pipes OpenAI models into Cursor, with direct model access ending November 12 — the latest date allowed by the change-of-control clause in the four-year agreement. The stated reason is trust: OpenAI "cannot be confident that SpaceX will use our technology within our terms of service," pointing at Musk's under-oath admission that xAI distilled OpenAI data in contract breach, and the Twitter enforcement record. Future models, including Astra, will not reach Cursor at all. The dominant coding front-end in the world just lost its default frontier model supplier because of who now owns it.

## Openness ended at the change-of-control clause, not at the firewall

**What happened.** OpenAI notified SpaceX it intends to wind down its Cursor contract, proposing November 12 as the hard-off date for direct access to OpenAI models, and confirmed future models including Astra will not be provided to Cursor. OpenAI cites the cancellation window in its custom agreement after a change of control and says it "cannot be confident that SpaceX will use our technology within our terms of service," citing Musk's April under-oath admission that xAI had violated OpenAI's terms (distilling OpenAI data to train models) and Musk's contract-breaking after buying Twitter, now part of SpaceX. The acquisition it reacts to: SpaceX folded in xAI in February, held a $60B purchase option on Cursor since April, agreed the $60B all-stock deal on June 16, and closed around mid-August — the largest acquisition ever of a venture-backed startup — and that cap-table event triggered the clause that severs Cursor's primary model supply line.

**Why it matters.** For anyone building on Cursor this is a 76-day clock: the GPT family — the tool's highest-end coding default — goes away on November 12, and the winner is decided by routing, not by benchmark. Anthropic's Claude is the obvious landing spot, Grok and Cursor's in-house Composer absorb the rest, and OpenAI's move reads as a bet that owning the model is worth more than owning the distribution. It also generalizes past this one deal: if the biggest coding front-end can be cut at a change of control because the new owner is a rival lab's parent, model access just became an explicit instrument in the vertical-integration war — and every platform that resells another lab's models now carries a contract risk on its cap table, not just its roadmap.

## What I'm watching

Whether Anthropic — whose Claude becomes the default frontier model inside Cursor by default — follows OpenAI's lead. Anthropic is simultaneously Cursor's model supplier and a SpaceX Colossus compute customer, so it holds both ends of the same conflict OpenAI just resolved on one side. That tension decides whether SpaceX-owned Cursor keeps any non-Grok frontier model at all.
