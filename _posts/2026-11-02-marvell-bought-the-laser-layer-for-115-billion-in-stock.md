Marvell and Lumentum signed a definitive agreement this morning for Marvell to acquire Lumentum in an all-stock transaction: 4.500 Marvell shares for each Lumentum share, an implied enterprise value of $115 billion at about $1,132 a share, a 23.9% premium to the last close before the takeover speculation began. Lumentum holders end up with roughly a third of the combined company. Closing is guided to the first half of 2028, subject to both shareholder votes and regulatory approval, so nothing changes at the contract level until then.

## The scarce layer got bought, not built

**What happened.** Both boards approved the deal unanimously. Marvell keeps Matt Murphy as chairman and CEO; three Lumentum directors, including CEO Michael Hurlston, join Marvell's seven-member board. The companies put the combined addressable market at $550 billion, expect more than $1 billion in annual run-rate cost synergies, and say the deal will be accretive to Marvell's non-GAAP EPS in year one after close.

**Why it matters.** The deal puts the two halves of the optical signal chain — Marvell's DSPs, retimers and switching silicon, Lumentum's lasers, transceivers and optical circuit switching — under one vendor that can sell a pre-validated end-to-end link. That helps time-to-market and hurts procurement leverage: fewer independent sources to qualify, and roadmap coupling that outlasts any one contract. The price is paid in dilution, not cash — Marvell is handing over about a third of the combined company — and the fixed exchange ratio leaves Lumentum holders long Marvell's AI multiple until 2028. The $1 billion of synergies is the tell: in a supply chain short of laser and optical capacity, the savings come from overlapping R&D and G&A, not from adding the capacity that made the deal necessary. The bet is that optics, not compute, is the next constraint — and that the laser layer is worth paying for in equity rather than building in-house.
