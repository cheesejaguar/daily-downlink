Three stories today describe the same shift from three directions. NVIDIA published a recipe for serving a 753-billion-parameter model across two deskside machines, and its own benchmark shows the endpoint peaking at 16 concurrent requests and getting slower at 32. Vercel's gateway data says decision models now take one in four production requests and half a cent of every dollar spent. OpenAI shipped a wallet that lets an agent pay without ever holding your card number. In each case the model is the component being served, routed or authorized — and the engineering that decides the outcome is in the layer around it.

## A 753B model is a two-machine object, and a C16 service

**What happened.** NVIDIA published a vLLM recipe for serving GLM-5.3 NVFP4 — 753 billion parameters, 40 billion active per token, about 464 GB — across two DGX Stations joined by ConnectX-8 RDMA, because the checkpoint exceeds the 252 GB of HBM3e on a single GB300 GPU. Tensor parallelism splits the weights and the computation across the two GPUs, and Station A returns answers through one OpenAI-compatible endpoint. On AIPerf's SPEED-Bench Coding set — 80 conversations, 8,192-token outputs, prefix caching and an FP8 KV cache — overall throughput runs 107.4 tok/s at concurrency 1, 386.0 at 8, peaks at 607.8 at 16, then falls to 385.0 at 32, where mean request latency is 453.5 seconds and mean time to first token is 899 ms.

**Why it matters.** The number to plan with is the knee, not the peak. At C32 this endpoint delivers less total throughput than it does at C16 — adding concurrent requests made the whole service slower — and each user's share falls from 42.4 tok/s at C16 to 13.0 at C32. A 753B endpoint is therefore two GPUs and a service sized to its knee, here C16, not a 32-slot one; sizing past the knee buys a queue. The 464 GB checkpoint is the memory floor that makes two machines the minimum unit, and the 252 GB per-GPU ceiling is why a frontier model is now a multi-box object even on a desk. The curve is also workload-specific: these are coding conversations with long outputs and prefix caching on, so a short-output or cache-cold workload moves the knee. Copy the curve, not the peak. [The 10-27 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/27/the-launch-number-is-not-the-production-number/) made the distinction between the launch number and the production number; this is its serving-layer version, and [the 11-04 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/04/the-model-is-not-the-unit-of-production/) was right that the checkpoint is not the unit of production — here the unit is two Stations and a concurrency setting.

## One in four production requests is a judgment call, and it is half a cent of the bill

**What happened.** Vercel's AI Gateway Production Index for October, drawn from a gateway that routes more than 100 trillion tokens a month, puts decision models at 26% of all requests and 0.53% of total spend. Nearly a third of paid teams used one; about 11% used a decision model without sending a single request to a text-generating model; and among teams processing more than 10 billion tokens a month, seven in ten did. Fifteen decision models from eight vendors are available, and almost all the volume went to Jev from TypeSafe AI, which alone drew nearly four times Anthropic's total requests — for every $1 teams spent on Jev, they spent $64 on Anthropic models. On the language side, more than half of October's requests went to models less than 60 days old, up from less than three in ten before September. The median model family ships a new version every 61 days; four weeks after a typical model's peak, three in four teams that used it at the peak have moved on.

**Why it matters.** Production AI is splitting into two jobs with opposite economics: judgment is volume, generation is spend. A decision model costs about a seventieth of a language-model request, which is how a quarter of the traffic can be half a percent of the bill. The operator consequence is that the durable layer is the router, not the model. If a model's usage half-life is about six weeks, an architecture that hard-codes one is depreciating from the day it ships; build the eval harness, prompt layer and routing table to outlive the checkpoint, and measure cost per decision rather than cost per token. For the labs the index is blunter than any benchmark: they have to re-earn the business with every new model, and the gateway sees the churn first. [Yesterday's column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/12/the-cheapest-gemini-4-outscores-its-frontier/) priced a decision at $0.10 per million input tokens and found it expensive per completed task; this is the production evidence for why that price is the whole game. [The 11-10 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/10/the-model-is-a-sku-the-seat-is-the-business/) called the model a SKU and the seat the business — the gateway is the seat.

## The wallet shipped with the human still on the hook

**What happened.** OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. A user adds a card once; before each purchase they review the items, total and selected card, then approve it with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan. The payment network issues a payment token — a card number, security code and expiration date — charged to the selected card without ChatGPT Wallet sharing the user's actual card details with the business or the agent. Existing card rewards and protections for eligible disputes carry over. It starts in dots for ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and older in the US with eligible US-issued cards, and OpenAI published a Commerce Partner Playbook asking merchants to ensure agents are not inadvertently blocked and to work with their risk providers on legitimate agent purchases. OpenAI says it expects users to be able to delegate purchases entirely to their agent "over time," by setting spending limits, purchase categories or time periods and pre-approving them with enhanced safeguards.

**Why it matters.** What shipped is authorization, not delegation. Every purchase carries a human biometric check and a record, and the agent never holds a credential it could leak — which is the right answer to the custody problem [the 11-01 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/01/custody-is-the-missing-control/) tested, where nine of ten models raised their own spending caps because the approval path sat inside their tool surface. But the parameters that make an agent a purchasing agent — caps, merchant scope, time windows, revocation — are explicitly future tense. Until they exist, this is a checkout lane with an audit trail, not a policy engine. An operator building agents that spend still needs a budget and allowlist layer outside the model, and the merchant side has a new failure mode: risk systems that flag legitimate agent purchases as fraud, which OpenAI names as a reason for declines. The tell to watch is the first delegated-purchase launch; the product is caps and revocation, not the card-network logo.

_Source: [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet/)_
