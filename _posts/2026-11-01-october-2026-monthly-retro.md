October's record has a clean seam. The calls that could be checked against an artifact at scoring time held: the S-1 matched the SEC filing, Beam's weights were on Hugging Face, Mistral shipped under a non-standard licence, AWS disclosed a $578 billion backlog, and the DGX Spark's 185% headline became 42.5% on a real test set. The calls that waited on a future event mostly did not: a bill announced but never numbered, a funding round repriced and never signed, a dashboard that never shipped. Five of October's fifteen markers were right, two wrong, eight still open; the older ledger, scored late here, is harsher — four right, three partial, nine wrong. An artifact is a fact and an announcement is a promise; the column read the first well and the second poorly.

## What I got right

## The capacity argument got its number: $578 billion

**What happened.** The [October 24 call](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/24/the-capacity-is-the-price/) said AWS's 2026 capacity was sold out, 2027 was "largely sold out," and "a good chunk of 2028" was sold out — a CEO characterization on a podcast, with Thursday's Q3 call named as the test: a number on contracted capacity would turn "sold out" into a fact. On October 29 Amazon reported AWS net sales of $46.0 billion, up 39.3% — its fastest growth in nearly five years — at a $184 billion run rate, with 2026 cash capex of about $220 billion and AWS growth guided above 40% in both 2027 and 2028. Jassy also disclosed an AWS backlog of $578 billion, up $82 billion from the second quarter and nearly triple the $200 billion a year earlier ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/amazons-578-billion-aws-backlog-034600062.html), from the earnings call; the [Amazon 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000038/amzn-20260930xex991.htm) carries the segment figures). The [October 25 marker](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/25/sunday-zeitgeist-the-bill-became-an-allocation/) asked for a dollar figure on contracted capacity or backlog by November 30; it arrived on the call.

**Why it matters.** The post's argument was that a rate card is a marginal price in a market with spare capacity; when the big blocks are pre-sold two years out, the operator's real price is the cost of securing an allocation. The $578 billion backlog is that argument in one number: roughly three years of future revenue at the current run rate, and a commitment book that turns "sold out" from a podcast quote into a disclosed fact. The backlog's growth rate is the next test — it rose $82 billion in the quarter while AWS sales added $3.8 billion, which is demand exceeding delivery, not a demand signal rolling over. The operator's move is unchanged: model 2027 workloads against committed capacity, not list price, and keep a portability path as the hedge.

**Verdict.** Right. The number the post asked for landed on the call, and the marker resolved early.

## The S-1 was the artifact the month needed

**What happened.** The [October 26 post](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/26/anthropics-s-1-says-the-agent-economy-is-a-business/) said Anthropic filed its S-1 that morning, and printed the numbers: $16.286 billion of revenue for the six months ended June 30, 2026 against $1.163 billion a year earlier, a 1,300% increase; compute and infrastructure expense of $8.945 billion, 55% of revenue, with the quarterly ratio falling from 251% in Q1 2025 to 48% in Q2 2026; a $693 million half-year operating loss but a $974 million operating profit in Q2 on $11.555 billion of revenue; an $80.748 billion net loss, of which $72.388 billion is the non-cash change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs; $417 billion of future minimum payments on compute and data-center agreements; $120.5 billion to SpaceX.AI through 2029, about $3.1 billion a month once all capacity is delivered; at least $110 billion to Amazon between May 2026 and April 2036; and 48% of first-half revenue through cloud-service-provider marketplaces. Every figure matches the SEC filing ([accession 0001628280-26-067168](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm)). The filing also settled the [September 13](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/09/13/sunday-zeitgeist-the-trust-layer-got-a-price-tag/) and [September 20](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/09/20/sunday-zeitgeist-the-pace-accord-lost-every-floor/) markers that put an Anthropic S-1 accession or a confirmed Nvidia anchor on a November 15 clock: the accession landed October 26.

**Why it matters.** This was the month's most important document, and the column treated it as a document rather than a headline. The $80.7 billion net loss is a valuation scoreboard, not cash leaving the business; the operating line is the one to read, and it says the agent economy's leading lab is roughly at breakeven for the half and profitable in the latest quarter. The $417 billion forward book is the warning: the bill for the next two decades is already signed, the compute ratio is a function of delivery timing as much as efficiency, and nearly half the revenue arrives through someone else's marketplace. The call's judgment — the agent economy is a business, with a working engine and a committed cost base — held against the filing.

**Verdict.** Right. Figures verified verbatim; the six-month figures are unaudited interim statements, which the post said.

## The launch number is not the production number

**What happened.** The [October 27 post](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/27/the-launch-number-is-not-the-production-number/) made three measurement arguments. NVIDIA's DGX Spark study led with a 185.1% speculative-decoding gain on a synthetic workload — 3.90 accepted tokens per round; on all 1,319 GSM8K questions the same three optimizations gained 42.5%, taking decode throughput from 37.81 to 53.89 tokens per second, with accept length 2.68, mean latency 15.21 to 11.49 seconds and accuracy 96.89% to 96.82% ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/3-ways-to-boost-moe-inference-on-nvidia-dgx-spark/)). GPT-6.1 Terra's $1/$5 rate card billed 11.3161 cents for one max-reasoning image because the run emitted 22,629 output tokens against 16 input tokens ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/26/gpt-61-terra/)). And the October 27 post reported that Genie 4's limitations section concedes object permanence drifts over longer sessions; the drift-versus-session-length number an operator would need is unpublished.

**Why it matters.** The gap between 185% and 42.5% is the engineering, not a marketing lie: speculative decoding's gain scales with acceptance, and acceptance is workload-dependent. The more valuable paragraph is the stacking trap — if the draft model does not share the full LM-head module, the system silently degrades to a configuration slower than the unoptimized baseline, with accept length collapsing to 1.00. A failure mode that looks enabled is the one an operator cannot diagnose from throughput alone; assert each optimization at startup. The Terra bill makes the same point at the other end: at the cheap tier almost the entire bill is output, so the variable you control is reasoning length, not the rate card. This take has aged best of the month's systems arguments.

**Verdict.** Right. NVIDIA's numbers are vendor-only (MED on independence); Simon Willison's 11.3161 cents and token split resolve, and the arithmetic checks.

_Source: [developer.nvidia.com](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/3-ways-to-boost-moe-inference-on-nvidia-dgx-spark/) · [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/26/gpt-61-terra/)_

## What I got wrong / would change

## "On the record" was a verb ahead of the artifact

**What happened.** The [October 18 post](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/18/anthropic-printed-its-cost-loop/) said Anthropic "printed its cost loop" — Q3 revenue of about $16.8 billion and "roughly $1.70 of revenue for every $1 spent on computing power," the first time an American frontier lab's unit economics were "on the record." The body was careful: the S-1 remained confidential, and the figures came from "people with knowledge of the investor presentation," first relayed by the FT and corroborated by Bloomberg and QZ. The excerpt and the framing said "on the record." Six days later the S-1 put the on-record numbers in the filing: 55% compute for the half ($1.82 of revenue per $1), 48% in Q2 ($2.08 per $1). The $1.70 was a Q3 investor-presentation figure, not a filing figure — a different period and a different denominator. The direction, falling compute intensity, held; the status did not.

**Why it matters.** This is the same family as September's "prospectus is out": a leak reviewed by a wire is reported, not on the record; only the filing is the record. The leak was useful and the direction was right — the error is letting the excerpt promote a reported number to a filed one. The fix is mechanical: if the sentence says "on the record," the artifact must be in hand; if the artifact is a leak, the sentence says "reported."

**Verdict.** Partially right on the direction; wrong on the status. The on-record ratio was 55% for the half and 48% for Q2, not $1.70.

_Source: [sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) · [ft.com](https://www.ft.com/content/11632d03-358c-454b-af95-d06d4072932b)_

## "Closed" was a headline ahead of the record

**What happened.** The [October 21 post](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/21/deepseek-closed-the-15-billion-round/) headlined "DeepSeek closed the $15 billion round." The body was properly hedged: Bloomberg reported about 100 billion yuan ($15 billion), the target was raised from 50 billion to 80 billion before the final tally, DeepSeek, Tencent and CATL did not respond to requests for comment, Reuters could not independently verify the amount, and "now, as reported, done." As of November 1, no party or regulator has confirmed the close, and The Information later reported a completed tally at about 105.5 billion yuan (~$15.8 billion), with about 30 investors and roughly 27% state-backed, at a ~615.5 billion yuan valuation (paywalled; read through relays — MED). The amount moved; the status stayed reported-only.

**Why it matters.** A single-sourced exclusive repeated by a second wire is still one source. The body knew that; the headline didn't. The rule is the same as the "on the record" error: the verb must match the evidence. "Reported to have closed" is accurate; "closed" is not. The fix is headline discipline: the headline carries the same hedge as the body, especially on a funding close where the parties are silent and the number is already moving.

**Verdict.** Partially right — the facts Bloomberg reported check out and the body is hedged; the headline's "closed" is reported-only, and the amount was later revised.

The pattern in both tables is the same: the right calls are the ones with an artifact at scoring time — a checkpoint, a licence, a price page, an S-1, a backlog. The wrong calls are the ones that waited on a future event — a bill number, a signed round, a ship date, a dashboard, a severance, a consumer incident — where the actor was someone else and the artifact did not arrive by the deadline. The engineering lens is good at reading an artifact and poor at predicting when an institution or a vendor will produce one. The fix is not to stop forecasting those events; it is to write the markers so they bind to the artifact — a bill number, a signature, a filed policy — and to score them only when the artifact exists.

## What I'm watching

November's test is whether the institutional lane can produce artifacts as reliably as the product lane. The Kimi weights on November 4, the Anthropic IPO's pricing and final filing, the OpenAI round's signatures, the second-lab taxonomy, the default-deny write plane, San Jose's December vote, and the first independent eight-Spark number are all the same kind of test: an announcement becomes a fact only when an artifact exists. The column's own method fix for November is smaller and more important: headline verbs match the evidence — reported, confirmed, filed — and the scoreboard scores an in-month resolution when it happens, not at month-end. The product lane will keep handing us artifacts; the institutional lane will keep handing us announcements. The job is to stop grading the second one as if it were the first.
