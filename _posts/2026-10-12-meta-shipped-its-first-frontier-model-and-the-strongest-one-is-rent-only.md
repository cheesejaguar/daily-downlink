Meta declared itself back in the frontier this morning. Muse Wonder and Muse
Wonder Full — the company's first models positioned as state-of-the-art agent
engines — claim comparable scores to GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's Fable 5.1,
ship today on the Meta Model API at $4/$20 per million tokens (about the
GPT-6 Sol / Opus 5.5 bracket), and were trained at Meta's Prometheus data
center in Ohio. The operator-relevant detail is who gets the whole thing:
Wonder Full, the stronger of the two, is not served through any API — it exists
only for Muse Code subscribers at $50–$200 a month, and the Muse consumer agent
rolls the model out first to its $20/$100 subscribers. The open-weights
champion's first frontier release since Llama 4 is closed, sold as rent.

## The most capable thing Meta made is not something you can buy by the token

**What happened.** Meta's Superintelligence Lab released Muse Wonder and Muse
Wonder Full on Monday, calling both frontier models with state-of-the-art
performance on personal and professional agent tasks, and Meta's model card
claims scores comparable to OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's Fable 5.1.
Wonder is live on the Meta Model API at $4.00/M input, $20.00/M output — a
price tier TechCrunch pegs at roughly GPT-6 Sol or Opus 5.5. Wonder Full is
Muse Code subscribers only (CNBC: $50 or $200 a month) and not on the API; the
Muse app takes the model first for its $20 or $100 subscription. Meta's stock
was up around 4% in afternoon trading.

**Why it matters.** The pricing read: a third frontier vendor now sits in the
same token-price bracket as OpenAI and Anthropic claiming comparable scores —
cheap to triangulate against, and the cheapest claim to file until an
independent run. The structural read lands harder: the lab that built its
reputation on open weights shipped its most capable models ever and made the
strongest variant reachable only by renting a code agent, not by token or by
weight. That is the opposite of [the 56% open-weights token share](/2026/10/12/the-inference-stack-is-unbundling/)
reported this morning — volume has moved to open, but Meta just bet the
*frontier* is worth more as a gated subscription than as gravity for an
ecosystem. The open-vs-closed axis just got its first genuinely new data point
of the week; the "who holds the frontier" answer is now three closed labs and a
claim to compare.

## What I'm watching

Whether third-party evals reproduce the Fable 5.1 / GPT-6 Astra parity — the
MemoryBench lead Meta touts is its own benchmark, and "active memory" maps
directly to the persistence fight this beat has been tracking all month. And
whether Wonder ever gets weights: the silence on that, from the lab that made
open weights its calling card, is the real signal to hold onto.
