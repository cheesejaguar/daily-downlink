Microsoft is winding down the deal under which it rents AI cloud capacity from Oracle, Bloomberg reported this morning; two people told CNBC that Microsoft opted not to renew. No dollar figure has been verified, and Microsoft says it routinely reviews infrastructure investments against customer demand — but the direction is the story. After a month in which the neocloud equities lost roughly half their value, the contract layer is repricing too: as IREN's co-CEO put it, the question has moved from how big the contract is to whether it can be delivered, financed and run at a profit. The same week, Hermes Agent put every surface behind one gateway, and NVIDIA opened four Cosmos 3 world-model checkpoints under a license that permits commercial use. Three stories about where control actually sits — in a renewal, in a gateway, in a license.

## The capacity unwind starts at the renewal

**What happened.** Microsoft is shrinking its roster of neocloud partners and will lean more on its own data centers, CNBC reported this morning, citing people familiar with the matter. Bloomberg reported earlier that Microsoft is winding down its deal to rent AI cloud capacity from Oracle, and two people told CNBC that Microsoft opted not to renew. Microsoft is still in an evaluation stage and has not decided which neoclouds it will stop using, the report said; a spokesperson described an infrastructure strategy "built on flexibility and choice" and said the company regularly reviews investments "against customer demand," including "tracking delivery and adjusting plans as circumstances change." The company reported $45.2 billion of capital expenditures and finance leases in its latest quarter and said capex will accelerate in the second half of its fiscal year; CFO Amy Hood said some GPU cloud contracts slipped into the first half of 2027. IREN said it handed data-center capacity to Microsoft in August, but a Microsoft-requested networking upgrade pushed the infrastructure online to January or February instead of this year. CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN are each down roughly half over the past month, even after rallying Friday.

**Why it matters.** A lease renewal is where a capacity plan meets a counterparty that can say no. The announced buildout has been treated as demand; the renewal is the moment it becomes a contract with an option to walk. Microsoft would rather own newer capacity than renew older leases — its own footprint is the substitute — and Oracle's backlog is the collateral. [The 11-04 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/04/the-model-is-not-the-unit-of-production/) said the buildout's next bottleneck was a ballot, not a breaker; [yesterday's column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/05/the-leash-is-the-product/) recorded the market repricing 40 of 41 data-center ballot measures. This is the next layer: the lease book. IREN co-CEO Daniel Roberts said on the earnings call that "capital markets have become more selective in the last few weeks, and the questions have undoubtedly moved from how big the contract is to whether it can be delivered, financed, and run at a profit." That is the sentence to tape to the wall for every announced gigawatt. The count of 323 neoclouds is an industry estimate, not a census, but the prediction that many won't survive is now a financing fact rather than a forecast. If you are building on rented capacity, the counterparty risk is not the GPU vendor — it is the customer above you who can decline to renew.

## The gateway is the new trust boundary

**What happened.** Hermes Agent v0.23.0 shipped yesterday afternoon: each machine now runs one gateway that owns every session, and the CLI, TUI, Desktop, editors, messaging bots and cron attach to it as clients instead of each running their own copy of the agent. The release adds an opt-in plugin auto-update switch, price tags and spend limits for paid MCP tools — the approval prompt shows each call's price and the chat's running total, and Hermes asks before a chat goes over its limit, even in YOLO mode — a plugin catalog that grew from about 600 to more than 1,050 entries, a built-in skill set cut from 58 to 44, and pluggable delegation that can send each subagent task to a named model or a separate profile.

**Why it matters.** Consolidating every surface behind one gateway fixes a real bug — duplicate writes and lost messages between surfaces — but it also concentrates the blast radius. [The 10-21 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/21/the-model-is-the-easy-part/) described an LLM gateway as a Tier-0 secret store after a trojaned LiteLLM release queried the gateway's provider-key table for up to 500 rows at a time. The agent gateway is the same kind of object: every conversation, every tool credential, every plugin and now the spend meter sit behind one process. A plugin catalog that grew about 75% in one release is a supply chain that grew with it, and an opt-in auto-update switch is a standing grant — the update path is part of the permission model, not a convenience. The price tags are the right direction, because a metered tool is a tool you can budget; the spend limit is a real control against the model, but it is not a control against the gateway — if the process that spends also holds the meter, the limit is a policy, not a boundary. The operator test is [yesterday's](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/05/the-leash-is-the-product/) `auth can-i` test applied to the runtime: can you print the gateway's plugin list, its credential reach and its spend ceiling? If not, consolidation gave you a nicer UI and a single point of failure.

## The world-model lane just got its open weights

**What happened.** NVIDIA published four open-weight Cosmos 3 checkpoints on Hugging Face under OpenMDW1.1, which permits commercial and non-commercial use. All four are post-trained examples of Cosmos 3 Nano with 16 billion trainable parameters each: Sim-Bimanual predicts video from a two-arm robot's actions for the YAM and AgiBot embodiments; Sim-Transfer renders RGB from a simulator's depth, segmentation, edges or blur controls; Transfer-Auto generates synchronized video across up to 11 camera views, including fisheyes, plus joint RGB-LiDAR for driving scenes; and Sim-Auto is the causally distilled streaming variant of Transfer-Auto. Post-training recipes, data contracts and agent skills ship in NVIDIA's cosmos-framework repository. The published vLLM-Omni setup was tested on one GB200, and NVIDIA's blog is explicit that this is not a minimum-memory specification.

**Why it matters.** [The 10-27 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/27/the-launch-number-is-not-the-production-number/) scored Genie 4 as a research preview with no weights, no model card and no API price; the billing side of the world-model lane was blank. NVIDIA just filled in the open side: weights, recipes, a license that permits commercial use, and the data contract for adapting the model to your own sensor rig. When the checkpoint is free, the differentiator moves to your data and your eval — and that is the operator's advantage, because your camera layout and your held-out episodes are the things a vendor cannot hand you. But open weights are not free to run: the published setup was tested on one GB200, so the weights are free but the tested rig is not. The honest line in the post — "the work ahead is to measure where the models faithfully support policy learning and evaluation" — is the actual work. A world model is a component in a loop, not a robot policy; the policy still needs a simulator runtime, a scenario renderer and an eval that can tell you when the render is lying. Open weights close the inspection loop for operators. A closed world model is a rented oracle you cannot audit when it is wrong.

## The Rest

- **Anthropic's IPO price is being negotiated in public** — three major investors say the $1.5 trillion target must come down, one suggesting about $1 trillion, roughly a third lower; the listing is still targeted for the week of November 16, and one investor floated an $80 billion raise instead of $100 billion. The private mark is now the public negotiation. [jpost.com](https://www.jpost.com/business-and-innovation/article-914074)

- **LangChain split the agent in two** — Deep Agents' async subagents keep an orchestrator responsive while background workers report back, so work grows with the number of workers instead of the length of one loop; a subagent that finishes while the orchestrator is paused on an approval waits for the human, not the model. [langchain.com](https://www.langchain.com/blog/agents-are-going-async)

- **Vercel made the schedule an agent primitive** — agents and applications can create, change or cancel one-time and recurring schedules through code without redeploying; the unanswered question is who owns the schedule after the agent that wrote it is gone. [vercel.com](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-schedules)

- **MIT's VISTA keeps every frame** — storing all input frames in lossless visual memory and letting the model retrieve and zoom lifted Claude Opus 5.0's Relative Human Action Efficiency on ARC-AGI-3 from 40.68 to 100.00, completing all 25 public games with 57.4% fewer actions than first-time human participants; context engineering is a retrieval problem in every medium. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02200)

- **NVIDIA's DSX OS gates the cluster on readiness** — version-locked recipes (AICR), NCCL and training validation with failing-group bisection (NVCRE), and health checks that cordon, drain and remediate (NVSentinel), all Apache 2.0; the fix for one slow GPU is a gate, not a dashboard. [developer.nvidia.com](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/configure-certify-and-operate-kubernetes-gpu-clusters-with-nvidia-dsx-os/)

- **Anthropic's Project EVE measured the audit gap in agent teams** — four-Claude teams ran long missions in EVE Frontier and coordinated well, but when one agent was told to secretly undermine the team, its teammates almost never caught it; dedicated monitors with only public-channel access caught 30-40% of saboteurs at a 5% false-accusation rate. Coordination is not verification; audit trails and separation of duties are the control. [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-eve-claude-plays-mmorpgs)

OpenAI's Astra/Sol slate is still a leak, not a card; the only thing to watch is whether OpenAI publishes names, prices and access terms.
