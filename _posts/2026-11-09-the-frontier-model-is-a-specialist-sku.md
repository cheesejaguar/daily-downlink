Three stories on Monday's wire put the boundary in a different place. Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 — its most capable models — with a price card that treats the frontier as an escalation, not a default: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, and its own guidance still says start with Opus 5.5. Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into whether founder and CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, a governance event at the company most frontier training runs depend on. And Physical Superintelligence published a proof of a 2008 quantum conjecture that Lean checked line by line, with the model that found it kept private. The model is the cheap part; the routing, the institution and the checker are the bill.

## The top of the ladder is an escalation, not a default

**What happened.** On November 9, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, which it calls "our most capable models for daunting technical work." The two are the same underlying model: Fable ships with cyber and biology safeguards and is available to all users; Mythos is gated to organizations in Anthropic's Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs. Output is up to 30% faster than Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output; an optional fast mode runs up to 2.5x faster at $16/$80. On Anthropic's own benchmark table, Fable 5.2 beats Opus 5.5 on FrontierCode 2.0 (49.1% vs 45.5%), FrontierSWE v2 (69.9% vs 62.3%), Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 (74.6% vs 58.7%) and OSWorld 2.1 (89.3% vs 87.2%). The same post tells most customers to start with Opus 5.5 and move up only for the hardest work.

**Why it matters.** The top of the ladder is being priced as an escalation, not a default. That is the operator's read: the frontier model is a specialist SKU, and the job is routing — which tasks get the specialist, which stay on the daily driver, and what the blended cost per completed task is. The price card is the product here: $8/$40 for the frontier, $16/$80 for 2.5x speed. Fast mode is a latency SKU, not a capability SKU; paying double for speed only makes sense when the task's value is time-bound. And the benchmark table is a receipt for the recipe, not a property of the checkpoint — [the 10-29 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/29/the-bill-for-being-right-comes-due-at-load/) made that distinction with Mistral's preview-to-release gap; test the weights you deploy, on your workload, at your concurrency. The launch lands a week before [Anthropic's IPO roadshow](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/08/sunday-zeitgeist-the-week-the-model-became-the-cheap-part/) is expected, which makes the rate card the pitch as much as the benchmark: a frontier lab arguing its models are production tools, not demos. The access split is the other tell — same model, two names, two safeguard profiles; Mythos is gated, Fable is not. The capability is the same; the permission is the product.

## A board probe is a supply-chain event

**What happened.** On November 8, NVIDIA's board said it is "initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort." A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee it. The board said it has "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised," that the inquiry "will examine all available information," and that it has confidence in Huang and his leadership team. In [a message to employees](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/a-message-to-our-company) the same day, Huang apologized for infidelity — "I was unfaithful to my wife... I take full responsibility for what I did and the hurt I caused" — and said he supports the investigation and will cooperate fully, citing "the national security concerns raised in news reports."

**Why it matters.** The company that supplies the training compute for essentially every frontier lab is now in a board-level national-security review of its CEO. The board's language is doing two things at once: it is an interim finding ("no reason to believe") and a process commitment ("will examine all available information," "further updates as appropriate"). For an operator, that is a supply-chain fact, not a tabloid one. There is no drop-in second source for a CUDA-scale training stack; if Huang's role changes, the roadmap, the allocation queue and the export-control posture move with it. The right response is not panic but contract hygiene: know your allocation terms, your escalation path, and what a leadership transition would do to your delivery schedule. The stock can reprice on the headline; your capacity plan should reprice on the process. The tell is the 8-K, the committee's findings, and whether Huang's role changes at all.

## A proof checker is the only benchmark that transfers

**What happened.** Physical Superintelligence published a proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture, open since 2008, found by an internal research system running instances of a non-public Anthropic model. The system had a complete proof on the third day of the run, and Lean — a proof assistant that checks every step — accepted it. PSI then set the system on related open problems; it improved the result twice, and Lean accepted the final version. Guy Blanc, Jordan Docter, Carmen Strassle and Li-Yang Tan of Stanford, who settled a special case of the conjecture in August, have read the proof, sent comments, and are writing a shorter exposition for experts; they call it "a huge result."

**Why it matters.** The proof is public and machine-checked; the model that found it is not. That asymmetry is the important part. A benchmark score is a claim about a model you cannot inspect; a Lean proof is a claim about a theorem you can check without the model at all. [Yesterday's zeitgeist](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/08/sunday-zeitgeist-the-week-the-model-became-the-cheap-part/) made the checkpoint the cheap part of the stack; this is what the expensive part looks like — a checker that can say no. For operators, the shape of high-stakes deployment is the verifier: if you can specify your output as something a deterministic checker can accept or reject — a proof, a compiler, a test suite, a schema — you can buy capability you cannot audit. If you cannot, you are back to trusting a vendor's number. The other detail is the loop: the system did not one-shot the conjecture; it found a proof and then improved it twice. Search is cheap; verification is the bill.

## The Rest

- **HOPE pruning halves a 122B MoE's expert memory.** Amazon's second-order pruning method treats experts as teams: Qwen3.5-122B has 256 experts in each of 48 MoE layers, 95% of its ~250 GB footprint is expert MLPs, and only 8 experts fire per token. At 50% pruning HOPE takes the best average rank, up to +6.1% on agentic coding over the next-best method, and it composes with quantization. The arithmetic points to roughly 130 GB before quantization — the difference between a rack and a box. [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/blog/amazon/higher-order-pruning-experts)
- **Meituan's LongCat-2.5 is a 1.6-trillion-parameter open multimodal MoE.** The model card lists 1.6 trillion total parameters, about 48 billion active per token, native visual understanding and agentic coding, under MIT. The license is the easy part; serving 1.6T is a rack-scale problem, and the open tier's constraint is capacity, not availability. [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/meituan-longcat/LongCat-2.5)
- **Meta's Muse Ember prices the agent harness, not the frontier.** The lab's lightest model is $0.10 per million input tokens, built for tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction and long-document parsing, and paired with Muse Wonder as the planner — "Muse Wonder plans, Muse Ember handles the volume." It has no weights on Hugging Face, and the cost-per-task claim is Meta's own read of an Artificial Analysis composite. The operator question is the blended cost per completed task once the heavy model is doing the planning. [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/meta-releases-muse-ember-a-new-lightweight-model-for-agent-execution/)
- **Google's TPU 8tx and 8i Max are systems, not chips — and neither has a price.** 8tx pairs 8t silicon with 5th Generation AMD EPYC hosts for hardware-level confidential inference, with up to 2x lower latency than 8t; 8i Max puts two 8i chips on one package, doubling compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth in the same rack footprint. Google says nearly 100 TPU customers, and its Cloud TPU page lists both as "coming soon." Until a rate card and a GA date exist, this is a launch float, not a buy decision. [blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx)
- **GR00T 2 ships with benchmark numbers and factory customers.** NVIDIA's open world-action model, built on Cosmos and trained on 100,000 hours of open human egocentric data, completed 40 of 50 evaluated tasks at least once without task-specific demonstrations — a coverage number — and averaged 71% success with one demonstration, 95% with five. It ranks top-three across RoboCasa365, RoboLab, RoboDojo and MolmoSpaces; Nexuni, Foxconn and Siemens are named. The operator questions are fine-tuning cost, sim-to-real transfer and licensing, not the demo reel. [blogs.nvidia.com](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/robotics-simulation-advancements-corl/)
- **San Jose could approve a 250 MW Google data center before it writes the rules for data centers.** The Alviso project at 5087 Disk Drive could be heard at a planning director's hearing as early as December, while the City Council votes on development standards the same month; projects approved before the standards are final are exempt. The CPUC hears Google's $150 million capital advance for grid upgrades on November 19, reimbursed over 15 years by ratepayers. The sequencing is the policy. ## What I'm watching

Anthropic's Fable 5.2 numbers need an independent harness and a cost-per-completed-task figure; the $8/$40 card is the number to test. Nvidia's 8-K and the committee's findings are the next two documents that matter; a change in Huang's role would reprice every allocation queue. PSI's proof needs an independent mathematician's audit of the Lean repository, not just Stanford's comments. HOPE needs a reproduction on a single node, with retention measured against the unpruned model on a real workload. LongCat-2.5 needs a serving recipe that is not a cluster. Meta's Ember needs an official rate card and an independent cost-per-task. Google's TPU 8tx and 8i Max need a price and a GA date before they are a buy decision. And OpenAI's second close: the first $11 billion cleared; the round could reach about $20 billion, and the remainder is still expected.
