Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon this morning — its strongest model, the company says, built for state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long-horizon work. Artificial Analysis independently puts it first of 52 models on its Intelligence Index at 59, one point above Claude Opus 5.5, with a 1M-token context window and text, image, speech and video input. Google's own numbers are sharper: 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for finding vulnerabilities, 54.8% on ExploitGym for exploitation, 71.0% on CWE-bench for patching, and +89 Elo to fourth on WebDev Arena at 1769. The price is Argon's — $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output during the introductory period, then $4/$20, with cached input 95% off. Availability is not: starting this week Carbon goes to a subset of cyber defenders, then trusted partners through the Fairwind Program, and only later to developers, businesses and consumers. This is [the Fairwind template](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/09/02/google-gates-a-cyber-twin-the-template-is-now-three-of-four-labs/) again, and the strongest model on the board is once more the one you cannot call.

## The frontier is handed out by trust tier

**What happened.** Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon today, two weeks after Argon, calling it its "next frontier model" and strongest model. It is proprietary, takes text, image, speech and video input, outputs text, and carries a 1M-token context window. Artificial Analysis scores it first of 52 on its Intelligence Index v5.0 at 59; Claude Opus 5.5 is at 58. Google reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, 54.8% on ExploitGym and 71.0% on CWE-bench; more than 2x uplift in secure-code generation over Argon; and 0.0% attack success on Gray Swan's indirect prompt-injection benchmark, against 0.7% for Argon. The company says it is taking part in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release testing. Availability starts this week with a subset of cyber defenders, then trusted partners through the Fairwind Program, then developers, businesses and consumers "as soon as possible." The introductory price is $2/$10 per million tokens, $4/$20 after the introductory period, cached input 95% off.

**Why it matters.** For an operator the number to watch is not 59; it is the ordering. Google's strongest model goes to defenders and vetted partners before it reaches an API, so the frontier you can rent is no longer the frontier that exists — security teams get the capability first, and everyone else gets a press release. The price did not move: Carbon launches on Argon's rate card, so this is a capability event, not a cost event, and the bill doubles when the $4/$20 list replaces the intro. What it breaks is the security baseline: at 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro and a $2/M introductory input price, autonomous vulnerability-finding is priced like a commodity input, and any code-review process that assumes a human finds the bugs first is now the slow lane. The caveat is the one that applies to every vendor benchmark: Google ran these without guardrails, so treat them as a ceiling until independent evals reproduce them — and watch for the API date, because that is when the rest of us get to test the claim.
