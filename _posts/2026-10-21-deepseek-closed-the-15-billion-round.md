Bloomberg reported tonight that DeepSeek has raised about 100 billion yuan ($15 billion) in its latest funding round — one of China's largest ever — with CATL and Tencent among the biggest investors. The target was raised at least twice, from 50 billion yuan to 80 billion before the final tally, according to people familiar with the matter; DeepSeek, Tencent and CATL did not respond to requests for comment, and Reuters could not independently verify the figure. The Chinese coverage puts the pre-money valuation at 500 billion yuan, roughly $75 billion, and says the company is preparing a STAR Market IPO. This is the close this column has been calling unclosed since October 6 — the round that spent three weeks "close to securing" is now, as reported, done.

## The price floor got a balance sheet

**What happened.** Bloomberg's Lulu Yilun Chen and Haze Fan report DeepSeek raised about 100 billion yuan ($15 billion) in its latest fundraising round, one of China's largest ever, after raising its target at least twice — from 50 billion yuan to 80 billion yuan before reaching the final tally. CATL and Tencent were among the biggest investors; the Chinese coverage adds industrial investors including Geely and SMIC-linked funds, and puts the pre-money valuation at 500 billion yuan (~$75 billion). DeepSeek, Tencent and CATL representatives did not respond to emailed requests for comment, and Reuters could not independently verify the amount. The company is preparing a STAR Market filing with CITIC Securities as sponsor, per the Chinese reporting.

**Why it matters.** DeepSeek is the lab that sets the open-weights price floor — the one the closed duopoly cut its own list prices against all year. For three weeks this round was "close to closing"; tonight it closed at $15 billion, and the constraint it relieves is capital, not compute. The money is earmarked for data centers and domestic silicon, and the round's real test is whether it converts into enough Huawei Ascend capacity to serve the volume DeepSeek prices for. If it does, the cheap-token floor is funded rather than subsidized and can outlast a price war; if it doesn't, this is a valuation event with a press release. Either way, the open lane's price-setter now has a balance sheet, and the next move to watch is not another fundraise but the chip order behind the next price card.
