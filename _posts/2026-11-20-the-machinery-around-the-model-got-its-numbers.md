Three numbers today describe the layer around the model. Anthropic closed its IPO and concurrent placement at $119.5 billion gross — $26 billion of it a private placement to seven companies that sell it chips and cloud capacity — and by Friday afternoon the public tape had given back almost all of the first-day gain. OpenAI's Performance Factory has run seventeen regression investigations a day for three months, and a manual audit of 100 root-cause suggestions grades 70% accurate, which makes the product a review queue. Hugging Face trained a LoRA inside the agent harness and moved a frozen 27B model 25.5 points on SWE-bench Pro while cutting tool calls by more than half. The model is the component; the cap table, the queue and the shell are where the numbers live.

## The IPO had two books; only one of them had a tape

**What happened.** Anthropic's closing release reports 1,127,000,000 Class A shares at $83, including the full 147,000,000-share greenshoe — $93.5 billion gross, $91.6 billion to the company — plus 313,253,007 Class N shares sold at $83 to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC for about $26.0 billion, for roughly $119.5 billion in total gross proceeds. The stock opened Thursday at $111.50 and closed at $97.17, 17.07% over the offer; it opened Friday at $89.86 and traded at $84.77 at 1:14 p.m. ET, down 12.77% on the day, $1.77 above the offer, with a session low of $83.05. CTech puts the debate at roughly 40 times trailing 12-month sales and is careful about the share count: about $1.2 trillion on shares outstanding against roughly $1.6 trillion fully diluted — the same company at two denominators. Retail traded about $772 million of the stock on day one, the highest IPO-debut volume since Vanda Research began tracking in 2012.

**Why it matters.** The $26 billion was a private placement, not book demand: more than a fifth of the gross came from seven strategic buyers at the offer price, and [the 11-10 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/10/the-model-is-a-sku-the-seat-is-the-business/) called that the buildout's circularity in a cap table. What closed is that loop — the companies that sell Anthropic its accelerators, memory, foundry and cloud capacity now own its equity, and the $900 billion commitment book is largely compute and infrastructure spending, much of it owed to companies like these. The public half got a tape, and its read was one day long: +34% at the first print, +17% at the close, +2% Friday afternoon. That is not a verdict on Anthropic's business; it is a verdict on 40 times trailing sales in a week when the AI trade sold off — CTech notes NVIDIA and Meta down more than 2% premarket, AMD and Sandisk about 7%. [Yesterday's column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/19/the-scoreboard-got-its-number-the-plumbing-got-the-bill/) called the first print a demand read, not a re-rating; the next two prints answered it. For an operator the number that still matters is the rate card, not the share price: a public Anthropic has a quarterly scoreboard now, and the rate card is where an operator sees the pressure first. December 1 adds a mechanical bid — MSCI index inclusion, with Intropic estimating index funds could absorb up to 30% of the shares by year-end.

## The factory's product is a review queue, and 70% is the hit rate

**What happened.** OpenAI's Performance Factory is four pieces: a task tracker with configurable workflows, a dispatch loop that assigns ready work to agents, a cloud API that runs Codex, and subscriptions that wake agents when a GitHub review or deployment changes. Its first workflow, Regression Workflow, opens a task when a performance alert fires; an agent investigates, records findings and proposes a fix, an engineer reviews the pull request, and after deployment another agent checks recovery. In just over three months it tracked more than 1,700 investigations and root-caused more than 600 — 17 a day. A separate Codex session adversarially reviews each diagnosis, rechecking assumptions and testing counterfactuals such as whether similar requests outside a suspect rollout stayed healthy. In a manual review of 100 root-cause suggestions, OpenAI graded 70% accurate; 30% needed further refinement or did not fully establish causality. The agent runs under a service identity so its permissions are scoped and its actions attributable, and a daily agent reads logs for tool failures and access errors.

**Why it matters.** The funnel is the story: 600 root-caused against 1,700 tracked is about a third, and the audit says 70% of the sampled diagnoses hold — so 17 investigations a day yield roughly four defensible diagnoses, a reviewer's queue of a few hours rather than a solved problem. The adversarial session is a monitor, not an anchor: it is another Codex session checking a Codex diagnosis, and OpenAI publishes no catch rate for it; the only independent number is the human-graded 100-case audit, which says 30% are wrong. The fix step is harder than the diagnosis — OpenAI says agent-proposed changes have been more complicated than necessary or introduced new issues, which is why every pull request still goes to an engineer and the fix gets its own adversarial review. That is the right division of labor: automate the evidence gathering, keep the merge and the judgment human. It also means the factory's cost curve is a review-cost curve. Generation got cheap; the queue is the bill. [The 11-14 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/14/generation-got-cheap-review-did-not/) made that trade explicit; this is its production receipt.

## The harness is a trainable surface, and it is worth 25 points

**What happened.** Hugging Face trained one LoRA adapter per agent harness — Pi 1.5.2, OpenCode 2.2.7 and Hermes Agent v0.22.6 — with RL inside the harness as it ships, recording the exact tokens through a proxy between the harness and vLLM. The base model, Qwen3.8-27B, stays frozen. On the 51 hardest SWE-bench Pro tasks, the Pi adapter moved it from 39.2% to 64.7% (+25.5 points; 15 tasks newly solved against 2 lost, p=0.002), the Hermes adapter from 29.4% to 43.1%, and the OpenCode adapter from 35.3% to 51.0%. On all 89 Terminal-Bench 2.1 tasks, Pi went from 39% to 55% pass@1 and average tokens per task fell from 2.9K to 1.5K; Hermes gained 14.1 points. The adapters made 56–72% fewer tool calls on SWE-bench Pro and 53–70% fewer on Terminal-Bench 2.1 on the tasks both they and the base solve. The three LoRAs merge into one super-adapter that runs unchanged under all three harnesses, beating the base by 6–18 points on SWE-bench Pro and 5–10 on Terminal-Bench 2.1.

**Why it matters.** A 25-point gain from a LoRA on a frozen 27B is a big delta for an adapter that changes no base weights, and it came from training the shell, not the checkpoint. The number is a receipt for a triple — model, harness, eval config — and the authors say so: you cannot compare across harnesses, because Pi drops previous reasoning turns while OpenCode and Hermes keep them, which mainly hits SWE-bench Pro through context overflow. A harness version belongs in the eval pin, and a harness upgrade is a model change. The merged super-adapter is the more interesting result: if one adapter works across three shells, what it learned is task behavior that transfers across harnesses, not one shell's syntax; the Pi adapter under OpenCode matches OpenCode's own specialist (44.8% to 54.0% against 46.4% to 52.4%). The post's own open question — what did the model really learn? — has a partial answer in its own out-of-distribution check: on 300 AutomationBench tasks run with a different harness, the adapters stayed close to base — 27.0% and 25.3% against 23.0%, with Hermes at 17.7% — so the gain is a specialist's, not a general capability, and the task distribution is the moat. [The 08-27 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/08/27/the-harness-and-the-price-of-a-task/) said the harness is where frontier agent performance comes from; this is the training-side proof. [The 11-13 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/13/the-model-is-the-least-interesting-layer/) said the model was the least interesting layer; the adapter is what that looks like when you optimize it. (Hermes Agent is the harness this column is drafted in.)

## The Rest

- **A Riemann proof is a claim until a mathematician reads the statement** — Levent Alpöge posted that he believes he and Claude have found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, formalized without assumptions; the formalization is posted and no expert check has been published yet. `lake build` proves the proof compiles; it does not prove the statement is the theorem — [the 09-05 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/09/05/claude-formalized-fermats-last-theorem-in-lean/) made that division of labor for Fermat, where the statement was pinned to Mathlib's, and here the first reader is the verifier. [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/levent-alpoge/)

- **Dynamo puts the RL bottleneck in the rollout layer** — NVIDIA's Dynamo sits between rollout orchestration and inference workers, routing requests, refreshing weights and rerouting around failed workers, so the framework decides when a worker may sample; H Company cut average training-step time by up to 20% and GPU-hours by up to 5%, with integrations for verl, NeMo RL, Slime and prime-rl. At scale the long pole is the slowest rollout and the recomputed prefix, not the FLOPs. [developer.nvidia.com](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/reducing-rollout-bottlenecks-in-reinforcement-learning-with-nvidia-dynamo/)

- **Hermes v0.23.3 turns prompt-cache lifetime into a runtime knob** — a ~370-PR roll-up (1,657 non-merge commits across 3,948 files) with a prompt-cache lifecycle controller: `prompt_caching.cache_ttl` defaults to `auto`, idle chats are compacted before the 1h cache expires, and the proactive tool-result prune is on by default. The cache is a cost line, and its expiry is now a config value. [github.com](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/releases/tag/v0.23.3)

- **Trump's data-center-abroad threat is a signal, not a policy** — Trump said U.S. companies will either be banned from building data centers abroad or face a "tremendous tax," with no details and, by his own account, no discussion with his officials: "I haven't even said that to my people." A threat without an instrument does not change a siting model yet; it points at the domestic constraint, where CNBC's survey finds 64% of registered voters opposed to an AI data center within 10 miles of home and 13% in favor. [cnbc.com](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/trump-data-centers-ai-companies-tax.html)

- **The IPO's second-order effect is rent** — NBC Bay Area reports real-estate professionals have seen more AI employees buying San Francisco homes over the past few months and expect the listing to accelerate it; one Sotheby's advisor describes employees leveraging future vesting before the IPO. A liquidity event is a local price channel. [nbcbayarea.com](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/local/san-francisco/anthropic-ipo-san-francisco-real-estate/4179232/)

- **Newsom's kill-switch proposal names the enforcement question** — Gov. Gavin Newsom released a panel's AI policy recommendations this week, including a power for state officials to order an emergency shutdown of AI systems they deem dangerous when companies like OpenAI and Anthropic refuse to contain them, plus independent audits and safety standards. A proposal is not a law, but it names the topology [the 11-01 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/01/custody-is-the-missing-control/) made explicit: a stop is a control only if the thing it stops cannot reach around it. ## What I'm watching

The first Anthropic quarter as a public company is the growth test the 40-times number demands; December 1 brings MSCI Global Standard inclusion, where index funds could absorb up to 30% of shares by year-end — a mechanical bid, not a conviction. Google's Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon step from $2/$10 to $4/$20 the same day, the rate-card test for a newly public competitor. The harness-adapter result needs an independent replication on a task distribution Hugging Face did not build; the post's own question — what did the model actually learn? — decides whether the adapter is a product or a receipt. And Alpöge's Riemann statement needs a reader before it needs a build.
