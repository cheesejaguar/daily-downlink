Two open-weight checkpoints are live this morning, and they point in opposite directions. Kimi K3.1 Pro is a 1.56 TB MXFP4 release — 2.8 trillion parameters with 104 billion active, a 1M-token context, 55 on the Artificial Analysis index against Opus 5.5's 58, at $2/$10 per million tokens, under a license that gates model-as-a-service use above $20 million in revenue. Ripple-65B Preview is a 65B dense model under Apache 2.0, built for a reported $32 million in full R&D, claiming 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1 and, Ricursive says, running on a DGX Spark. Both are open; only one fits on a desk. The rest of the day's work was below the model: NVIDIA published a loop that turns production failures into tests and harness fixes, with held-out cases the proposing agent never sees; AI2 published a benchmark showing long-context models can retrieve a fact and still lose a chain of state changes; and the midterms turned data-center permitting into a voted variable, with 33 local measures to restrict data centers passed or leading late Tuesday and only one pro-data-center measure winning among those tracked. The model is not the unit of production; the recipe, the harness, the state and the permit are.

## A 65B model just made the open frontier a recipe problem

**What happened.** Ricursive Intelligence released Ripple-65B Preview, a 65B-parameter dense Transformer pretrained on 12 trillion tokens with support for up to a 1M-token context, under Apache 2.0 in BF16 and NVFP4 builds. The model card claims 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1, above Reflection Beam 501B (80.1), Meta Muse Spark Open 816B (76.4), Thinking Machines Inkling 975B (63.8) and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B (56.4). Ricursive says the full R&D cost from scratch — pretraining-ladder development, data mixing, evaluations, synthetic data, post-training recipe and the final run — was $32 million, which it calls one-tenth the previous open-weight SOTA's estimated cost. The model is on Hugging Face, Together, GMI Cloud and OpenRouter; Fireworks goes live later today. OpenRouter lists it at $0.20/$0.60 per million tokens with a 16,384-token context, not the 1M the card claims, and the card says its small parameter envelope lets it run on a DGX Spark.

**Why it matters.** The first thing to say about 80.52 is that it is a vendor's number on a vendor's card, and the comparison figures come from the same card; treat it as a claim with a receipt, not a result. If it holds, the claim is the story: a 65B dense model built for $32 million beating 501B, 816B and 975B open mixtures-of-experts on agentic coding says the open frontier is a recipe problem — data, architecture and post-training — not a parameter-count problem. The operator number that matters more is the serving envelope: 65B dense in NVFP4 is roughly 35 GB, which fits a single 48 GB card, and Ricursive says it runs on a DGX Spark; that is a checkpoint you can own and serve, not a 1.56 TB artifact that needs a cluster. The first OpenRouter listing serves it at 16,384 tokens, not the 1M the card claims, which is [the 10-27 column's](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/27/the-launch-number-is-not-the-production-number/) launch-number-versus-production-number trap in miniature: ask what the provider serves, not what the checkpoint supports. And the $32 million is a full R&D cost from scratch, self-reported; if it survives scrutiny, it resets the entry price for an open-frontier lab.

## The reliability gain was a prompt edit, not a weight update

**What happened.** NVIDIA's November 4 technical post walks one complete optimization cycle on a Hermes enterprise assistant running in an OpenShell sandbox. NeMo Compass read 42 stored traces against `ETHOS.md`, a file that defines the assistant's expected behavior, and found the agent calling `send_message` without user confirmation. NeMo Eval Author then wrote four Harbor evaluations, two of them held out from the coding agent proposing fixes. The harness change was a single `SOUL.md` edit: requests to write, draft, compose, prepare or suggest a message authorize preparation only; provide the draft and stop before sending. On the development set of two tasks run three times each, passes went from 3/6 to 6/6 and tool calls from 10 to 6; on the held-out set, passes went from 4/6 to 6/6 and tool calls from 28 to 15. The same loop on a Dassault Systèmes car-door workflow cut malformed tool calls from 142 to zero and produced a 28% relative gain in overall evaluation score with 35% fewer tool calls across six scenarios, running overnight in about 12 hours without human intervention.

**Why it matters.** The change that produced the gain was a prompt edit, not a checkpoint. That is the harness thesis [the 10-30 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/30/the-harness-is-the-product-and-the-bill-is-the-depreciation/) made when it called the harness the product; this is the same claim with a measurement attached. The discipline that makes the measurement credible is the held-out set: two tasks were withheld from the coding agent proposing the harness change, and a change had to improve both sets. Without that, an agent optimizing against tests it can see is overfitting with extra steps. The sample is small — two tasks, three runs each, six runs per set — so 3/6 to 6/6 is a receipt for a recipe, not a distribution; [the 10-27 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/27/the-launch-number-is-not-the-production-number/) made the distinction between a launch number and a production number, and six runs is a launch number. The operator question is whether your agent leaves traces complete enough, and evaluations specific enough, to run the loop at all: most teams have logs, very few have a held-out suite and a verifier that marks a send-without-confirmation run as a failure. The Dassault result is the one to watch — 142 malformed tool calls to zero is not a prompt trick; it is a validation step added after Compass found the same misplaced argument across multiple traces, and the loop ran unattended overnight. The review question moves with the change: if the harness edit lands in a repository, the approval has to name who signs it and what stops the loop from optimizing a metric that is not the job.

## The buildout's next bottleneck is a ballot, not a breaker

**What happened.** Wednesday's post-election tape split the AI trade: the Dow was up 1,178 points, or 2.3%, by 9:35 a.m. ET, with the S&P 500 up 1.2% and the Nasdaq 1.1%, while Nvidia fell 1.5% as the heaviest weight on the S&P and First Solar jumped 15.1% and Enphase Energy 12.1%. The vote count explains the split. Stateline reports that 33 ballot measures to restrict data centers had passed or were leading late Tuesday, with roughly a dozen more in communities whose totals had not been announced; the measures ranged from outright prohibitions and moratoriums to energy-use limits and requirements for voter approval before construction. Only one measure, in North Dakota, produced a win for data-center supporters among the measures tracked. In Ohio alone, 21 municipalities voted on restrictions. An NBC exit poll found 56% of voters called AI and data centers a crucial or major factor in their congressional vote, and a UMass Amherst poll from earlier this year found 11% of Americans would support an AI data center in their community.

**Why it matters.** For an operator, the constraint on the 2027 capacity queue is no longer the turbine, the transformer or the GPU; it is the local ballot. A moratorium adds years and a lawsuit to a project, and a requirement for voter approval turns a site into a political campaign. That changes the cost of capital for the buildout: [the 10-24 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/24/the-capacity-is-the-price/) said capacity is the price; a permit is now a term in that price, and the market is already marking it — AI stocks under pressure, solar and wind up on the same day. The voters are not rejecting AI in the abstract; 56% called it a factor in their congressional vote and only 11% want a data center next door, which is a siting and cost-allocation problem, not a model problem. Operators who plan capacity as a lease signed with a utility are planning against the wrong counterparty; the counterparty is a city council that can be voted out. The buildout's unit economics now include a political discount rate, and the projects that survive will be the ones that bring the power, water and tax terms a community will actually vote for.

## The Rest

- **Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights are live, and the license is the new gate** — Moonshot published the full checkpoint on Hugging Face this morning: 96 shards, 2.8 trillion parameters with 104 billion active, a 1M-token context, $2/$10 per million tokens with a $0.20 cache read, and 55 on the Artificial Analysis index against Opus 5.5's 58. The release is quantization-aware MXFP4 weights with MXFP8 activations, not a BF16 reference — 1.56 TB for 2.8 trillion parameters is about 4.5 bits per parameter, and that is the serving format. The custom license is free for use, modification and distribution, but a model-as-a-service business above $20M in aggregate revenue over any consecutive 12-month period needs a separate agreement, and products above 100M monthly active users or $20M monthly revenue must display the model name. [The 10-23 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/23/kimi-k3-1-pro-is-fourth-and-the-weights-are-a-promise/) said the weights landing by Nov 4 at the claimed score would move the open frontier within three points of the closed one in one step; they landed, and it did. [This morning's breaking note](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/04/kimi-k3-1-pro-weights-came-due-at-1-56-terabytes/) has the artifact read. [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)

- **Long context retrieves the fact; it does not hold the state** — AI2's StateBench derives four difficulty levels from the same sequence of variable assignments and tests the same variables at the same update points, changing only the type of assignment; pretrained transformers held the easiest, lost accuracy on aperiodic ones as inputs grew and sat at chance on general finite, while Gemini 3.8 Flash, Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna fell to 13% or lower on the longest general-finite cases. Format mattered: 275-million-parameter hybrid and Gated DeltaNet models were near ceiling on RULER-style framing and near chance on spreadsheet edits. [The 11-02 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/02/the-bill-arrived-itemized/) made the point that a clean scan is evidence about its probe set; a retrieval eval that never asks about state is the same kind of zero. [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/statebench)

- **Meta bought the coding-agent control plane** — Conductor, the startup that lets engineers run and manage a team of coding agents in the cloud, announced it is joining Meta and will shut down Conductor Cloud on November 20, keeping its local Mac app. The target is not another model lab but the surface where a person supervises several agents at once — the same layer [OpenAI is buying for Codex](https://openai.com/index/openai-to-acquire-pierre) with Pierre, and the same layer [the 10-30 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/30/the-harness-is-the-product-and-the-bill-is-the-depreciation/) called the harness. [conductor.build](https://www.conductor.build/) · [unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/conductor-joins-meta-and-will-shut-down-its-coding-agent-cloud/)

- **OpenAI is buying the storage layer under Codex, not another lab** — it agreed to acquire Pierre Computer Company, the team behind Code Storage and the Trees and Diffs libraries already used in the Codex app; terms were not disclosed and the deal is subject to regulatory approval. The announcement follows Pando, the Git backend [the 11-03 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/03/the-demo-is-the-cheap-part/) covered, and the logic is the same: agent push volume, not human commit volume, is the load the code host was not designed for. [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/openai-to-acquire-pierre)

- **The single-model stack is over, and the bill is why** — OpenRouter reports that customers spending $100 or more a month now use a median of six model families, and $10K+ accounts used 12 labs in October; Kilo's own dashboard shows no model taking more than 30% of any agent role, with frontier models concentrated in review, where Claude Opus takes 11.5% and GPT models add 14.4%, while gpt-6-luna is the only frontier model in code's top 10 at 1.4%. Revolut's AI operations lead says the bank runs GLM 5.3 on about 56 B200 GPUs at roughly 30 cents per million tokens — one tenth its Anthropic and OpenAI bill — and measured 70% better lead time and 60% faster coding after moving to self-hosted GLM 5.1; a flat bill stopped engineers rationing tokens. [blog.kilo.ai](https://blog.kilo.ai/p/the-model-mix-is-growing-and-moving)

- **NVIDIA's factory blueprint is an agent org chart with an approval matrix** — FOX puts a factory manager agent over specialist inspection, root-cause and fleet agents connected through MCP, with per-decision operator review, and can run fully on-premises on a DGX Station so prompts, images and production records stay inside the factory network. At Siemens' Erlangen electronics plant, rush-order simulations through the blueprint are credited with up to 20% cost savings, and virtual production preparation with up to 40% faster time to market. ## What I'm watching

The OpenAI round is still about $11 billion secured against a $20 billion target at a $1.25 trillion valuation, and it is still not closed; signatures and a final size are the event, not another revision. Nvidia–Reflection remains an armed watch with no closure. The numbers I want next are independent ones: a reproduction of Kimi's 55 and Ripple's 80.52 on the artifacts themselves, not the vendor cards, because an open checkpoint that only its maker has measured is a promise in a serving format. And after Tuesday's ballot measures, the 2027 capacity queue is being set by city councils; watch which projects bring a power and tax package a community will vote for, and which ones discover that the permit was the schedule all along.
