Yesterday handed us the week's split screen: Microsoft claimed a 2.8-trillion-parameter base train [nobody can run yet](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/15/microsoft-mai-base-2-a-frontier-datapoint-not-a-product/), while Nvidia put a 24GB open model on a box going on sale today. The small end just delivered. RTX Spark is on sale from $2,599, the Nemotron Bolt weights it was built to run are on Hugging Face, and the first independent numbers from a real box are in — honest, which is to say laptop-class. The same 24 hours produced Meta's 816B open MoE, and its most instructive details are the terms around it: the hardware you must buy to serve it, and the revenue trigger hiding inside "open."

## The receipt for the local-open bet is in, and the number is laptop-class, not miracle-class

**What happened.** RTX Spark machines went on sale today across Microsoft, ASUS, Dell, Lenovo and MSI, from $2,599, built on Nvidia's N1X — a 20-core Grace CPU with a 6,144-core Blackwell RTX GPU and up to 128GB of unified memory, at 45–80W in a laptop. The Bolt 74B-A9B weights it targets, sized for 24GB, have been on Hugging Face since release, with quant variants refreshed this morning. The first independent measurements landed in the same press cycle: on Artificial Analysis's agent workload, an RTX Spark laptop finishes the Qwen3.8-27B run in 18.7 minutes versus 17.3 on the DGX Spark and 37.6 on a MacBook Pro — against 4.9 on an RTX 5090 desktop. On the lighter Qwen3.6-35B A3B workload the pattern holds (7.6 minutes, near the DGX Spark's 7.3).

**Why it matters.** This is the score of the argument I made yesterday: don't buy the 74B headline, measure the active-parameter cost on a real box. [The numbers](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/15/open-weights-became-a-24gb-download/) make the local tier credible in one direction and honest in the other. Credible: near-DGX-Spark agent throughput on hardware you own, at a laptop price — the open loop really does close on a machine you can buy. Honest: the same workload runs almost four times faster on a discrete-GPU desktop, and for agent work the number to watch is not decode speed but prefill. A cold long prompt costs minutes before the first output token, which is exactly when a long-horizon agent starves. Nvidia lists Bolt first in its class on PinchBench and second on BrowseComp — those are vendor-published quality claims; the independent half of the receipt is the run cost, and that you can now reproduce yourself. Judge the box by prefill at the context length your agents actually hold, not by the laptop brochure.

## Meta's 816B is open by download and closed by fine print

**What happened.** Meta released the weights of Muse Spark Open, an 816B-parameter mixture-of-experts model activating 42B per token, with a 1M-token context window and native image, video and audio input. It is on Hugging Face, SageMaker, Google Cloud Model Garden and Microsoft Foundry, and served by Baseten, Fireworks, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter and Together. Artificial Analysis scores it 39 on its Intelligence Index — the top mark yet for a US open-weight model. The less glamorous details carry the actual story. Its reference deployment is two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios over MLX, each with up to 512GB of unified memory, because an 816B model needs roughly a terabyte of memory to serve. And the Muse Model License is conditional: "Built with Muse Spark Open" attribution is required, and model-as-a-service businesses above $20M in annual revenue must sign a separate agreement with Meta.

**Why it matters.** Four days after Meta shipped its most capable frontier model as [rent-only](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/12/meta-shipped-its-first-frontier-model-and-the-strongest-one-is-rent-only/), it re-entered open weights with a launch that turns "open" back into a ladder of terms. There are two prices hiding in this card that have nothing to do with quality. The hardware price: the flagship deployment is a pair of Mac Studios, not a laptop — at 816B, the run-it-yourself story only works if you already own a workstation cluster. The commercial price: below $20M ARR in MaaS the weights are free to use; above it they stop being open and become a negotiation. For the people building on these things, that distinction is the whole game — downloading 816B of weights is satisfying and nearly useless if the terms cap the business you can legally build on them. Nvidia's Bolt, a fraction of the parameters and open all the way down to data and recipes with no revenue trigger, is the sharper point of comparison on the very same day. And the Hugging Face card dates this checkpoint to mid-September: a month-old model announced as the US open-weights leader tells you how far ahead of announcement Meta thinks it can afford to be.
