Three stories this morning, one read: the inference stack is being unbundled from whoever sold you the model. Microsoft said Azure Maia 300 becomes the primary inference system for its own products by 2027, scaled to multi-gigawatt deployments at 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU systems — the largest buyer of AI silicon telling the market its inference is going home. Vercel's production index put the second number on record: open-weight models carried 56% of gateway token volume in August, up from 7% in December, because they keep getting cheaper and keep being good enough. And the skills registry crossed a million listings in seven months. [Last week's read](/2026/10/11/sunday-zeitgeist-the-bill-moved-off-the-rate-card/) said the bill moved off the rate card; this week's spotlights say the bill is now splitting across three owners — silicon, tokens, and judgment.

## Inference is going home to hyperscaler silicon

**What happened.** Microsoft announced Azure Maia 300, its third-generation in-house AI accelerator, with production starting in 2027, and said it will scale the Azure Maia family to multi-gigawatt deployments starting that year, making Maia "the primary AI inference system for Microsoft products and services," in the words of EVP Scott Guthrie. Microsoft expects Maia 300 to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems and, in a full 96-accelerator configuration, up to 7x the rack-scale token throughput of Maia 200. Maia 200 is already in production running Copilot in the US, Europe, and Australia, with GitHub Copilot and Foundry following by year's end.

**Why it matters.** This is de-risking at the source. Microsoft buys more AI silicon than almost anyone, and it is now telling its suppliers — Nvidia first among them — that inference for its own product line is going home to chips it designs. That is the [profit-per-gigawatt logic](/2026/10/09/vllm-rubin-nvl72-inference-preview/) from Friday, applied as procurement: when the biggest workload is inference, the margin lives in tokens pushed per megawatt, not in rent on GPUs. The operator read is a pricing signal, not a rumor. When the biggest buyer internalizes the inference layer, the floor under everyone else's inference bill moves down, and the weekend's [Nvidia–Reflection courtship](/2026/10/11/the-agent-breakout-reports-share-one-diagnosis-persistence/) starts to look like securing the training-and-serving bottleneck that a de-Nvidia'd hyperscaler no longer provides. Custom silicon at hyperscale was a hedge twelve months ago; Guthrie's post turns it into a product plan with a date.

## Open weights took the volume; the spend stayed put

**What happened.** Vercel's AI Gateway September Production Index, covering traffic through August, shows open-weight models carrying 56% of token volume — a majority for the first time, up from 7% in December. Price per token fell another 23.2% in August, its third straight monthly drop, and among teams running more than ten million tokens in both months, the median team paid 7.6% less. The spend picture is more instructive: Anthropic still took 64% of gateway spend, and while Fable 5, its most capable model, fell from 13.2% to 4.9% of that spend, Opus 5 rose to 22.5% — the flight was to a cheaper tier of the same lab. Google came out worst: Gemini 3 Flash lost 95% of its token share since May as Google's gateway share fell from 30% to 5%.

**Why it matters.** This is the production datapoint the [open-vs-closed argument](/2026/10/05/the-open-weights-race-finally-has-a-name/) has been waiting for: a majority of real token traffic now runs on weights you can audit, host, and fork, and the engine is price, not ideology. Open weights win volume because they are good enough and the unit price keeps falling; labs keep spend because an expensive wrong answer still justifies a premium. The [two-leaderboard picture](/2026/08/30/the-advisory-is-the-attack/) this column drew in August is no longer a thesis, it is a chart. For an operator the design consequence is concrete: default the high-volume lanes to open weights behind a gateway that routes premium tasks to a frontier model — the traffic is already telling you that is the stable configuration.

## A million skills is the layer that makes an agent yours

**What happened.** Vercel's "State of agent skills" report puts the skills.sh registry at one million agent skills and roughly 280 million installs in seven months — faster than GitHub reached a million repositories (27 months), the App Store (63 months), or npm (117 months). Supply leans technical, with software engineering the largest category of listings, but more than four-fifths of installs go to other kinds of work, and cross-industry skills are 66% of classified listings yet 87.5% of installs, averaging 3.6x the installs per listing of industry-specific ones.

**Why it matters.** Skills are the portable-judgment layer: the difference between a generic agent and one that knows how your team does its job, written down once and installable anywhere. The [refactor postmortem](/2026/09/20/the-19k-refactor-a-defense-and-a-countdown/) this column covered in September showed the compounding loop — a usable skill grew out of the session and became guidance for every later run. A million listings means that loop is now an ecosystem, and the surprising stat is the allocation: the biggest demand is not the engineering skills supply favors, it is cross-industry judgment — the report's line that the first million skills taught agents what everyone knows, the next million will teach each agent what only you know. Owning the skill layer is how a team stops renting its process along with its model.
