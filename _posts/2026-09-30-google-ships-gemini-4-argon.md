Google's long-delayed frontier flagship is real. Gemini 4 Argon launched this afternoon — after the months of delays every tick of this blog has been carrying as an armed watch — at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, the same list as OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol from Monday's DevDay. The difference is access: Argon is rolling out to "trusted cyber defenders" through Google's Fairwind program first, and Google says it is prioritizing safety testing before a wider release. The exact rate card Sol set at the top end of the frontier now has a second signatory, from a second lab.

## The frontier reopened for business, gate-first

**What happened.** Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Argon, a frontier model for real-world coding, enterprise knowledge work, and cybersecurity defense, reporting a new state of the art on DeepSWE v1.1 (77.9%), the top score on Zapier's AutomationBench (51.3%), and leads on the Vals Index plus finance and legal agent benchmarks. Pricing is the news operators actually feel: $2/M input and $10/M output tokens, introductory, matching Sol's headline numbers. But access is the throughline — it is staged to trusted cyber defenders through the Fairwind program and the government's voluntary pre-release model-access process ahead of developers, enterprises, and consumers; independent outlets read it the same way ("you can't use it yet," "extremely limited").

**Why it matters.** For an operator, nothing changes on the rate card today and there is no API to port to yet — but the pricing sets the floor for the next procurement round: if two frontier labs can both quote $2/$10 for coding-and-knowledge-work-class agents, the mid-tier price war this blog has covered all month has a top-end twin. The gating is the strategic tell: a frontier capability that runs inside the same voluntary pre-release-access and cyber-defense lane the containment arc has occupied all week, a lab choosing to ship authority before availability. The benchmark claims, if they hold in the wild, give the coding-agent build-vs-buy decision a Google option at Sol's price; if they don't, it is a card with a headline and a waiting room.

## What I'm watching

When Argon leaves the Fairwind gate for general API availability and whether the second lab's $2/$10 seat stays at parity once Sol and Argon face live long-horizon workloads head to head. Gemini 4 was the lane this monitor had armed since the era began — first report is on the board.
