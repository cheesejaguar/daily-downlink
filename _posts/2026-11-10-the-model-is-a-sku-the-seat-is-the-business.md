Anthropic filed the price range for its IPO this morning: 825 million Class A shares at $70–$78 on Nasdaq, a $26 billion private placement taken by the companies that supply its compute, and a $900.9 billion commitment book against $33.2 billion of nine-month revenue. Google opened Gemini 4 Carbon to every paying developer at $2/$10 until November 30, then $4/$20. And Anthropic shipped an office suite the same morning — Claude Meetings, Sheets and Whiteboards — from the company that still books 44% of revenue through other people's marketplaces. [Yesterday's column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/09/the-frontier-model-is-a-specialist-sku/) called the frontier model a specialist SKU; today the price of the company, the price of access, and the price of the seat are the business.

## The IPO has a price; the commitment book is the bill

**What happened.** Anthropic's S-1/A, filed this morning, sets an estimated $70–$78 per share for 825 million Class A shares on Nasdaq under "ANT" — 801.4 million new shares and 23.6 million from selling stockholders — plus a $26 billion concurrent private placement of Class N stock at the IPO price, bought by AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC, with Google and Nvidia taking $10 billion each. The filing reports $17.0 billion of Q3 revenue and $33.2 billion for the nine months, a $104.0 billion net loss of which $94.3 billion is the change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, a $1.26 billion GAAP operating loss against $4.08 billion of adjusted operating income, and $900.9 billion of purchase commitments and leases — substantially all compute and infrastructure, $451.6 billion of it expected before 2031 and $70.7 billion due inside a year. The Q3 compute ratio rose to 58% from 48% in Q2 as capacity landed, including $5.0 billion of short-term compute; the placement shares are unregistered and outside the underwriters' lock-up.

**Why it matters.** Two numbers carry the operator story. The first is $900.9 billion of commitments against $33.2 billion of nine-month revenue: the revenue is real, but the bill for the next two decades is largely contracted, and $70.7 billion of it comes due inside a year. The second is the 58% compute ratio — the margin that fell to 48% in Q2 rose again when a block of capacity landed, which is [the 10-26 column's](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/26/anthropics-s-1-says-the-agent-economy-is-a-business/) point that a margin rented from a fixed cost base is a schedule, not an efficiency. The $104 billion net loss is mostly a valuation scoreboard: $94.3 billion of it is the fair value of the notes rising with the enterprise value, not cash leaving the building; the operating line — $1.26 billion GAAP loss, $4.08 billion adjusted — is the one to read. The $26 billion placement is the structure to watch. The suppliers of Anthropic's compute are buying its equity at the IPO price: that is the buildout's circularity in a cap table, not a scandal, but a supply overhang that arrives before the insider lock-up expires. One more line from the filing: the $41.3 billion Lambda agreements include a $34.9 billion tranche that is currently terminable by either party because Lambda missed a facility-lease deadline — a reminder that a commitment book is not a floor ([the 09-01 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/09/01/nvidia-holds-the-lease-behind-anthropics-35b-lambda-deal/) covered the lease behind it). The filed range is the fact; the trillion-dollar marks are arithmetic on a fully diluted share count. [TechCrunch's](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-sets-ipo-price-range-of-70-to-78-per-share-to-reach-an-implied-valuation-of-1-404-trillion/) $1.404 trillion is its own sum, and [the 09-19 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/09/19/the-frontier-priced-its-future-decisions-priced-at-zero/) recorded the $2 trillion target this filing came in under.

## The gate opened, and the price has an expiry

**What happened.** Google made Gemini 4 Carbon available through the API in Google AI Studio and Gemini for business to every paying developer and enterprise, and to all Google AI Ultra subscribers in Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. The rate card is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output through November 30, then $4/$20. Google says Carbon ranks first on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench at more than 2x lower cost per task than the next-best external model, and second on the new Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark at more than 6x lower average cost per task. In Antigravity it previewed `/swarm`: a coordinator that spins specialized workers into parallel isolated git worktrees so edits never collide, with adversarial verifiers challenging every diff and all agents posting to a shared, append-only board, parking at zero token cost when idle.

**Why it matters.** The [10-28 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/28/gemini-4-carbon-is-googles-strongest-model-and-defenders-get-it-first/) ended by saying to watch the API date, because that was when the rest of us could test the claim. This is the date, and the claim now has an expiry: $2/$10 until November 30, $4/$20 after. That makes the next three weeks a trial of Google's "2x lower cost per task" number on your own workload, and December 1 the date your budget has to assume the list price. The vendor composites are a ceiling until reproduced; [the 11-03 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/03/the-demo-is-the-cheap-part/) made cost per completed task the number that survives a benchmark, and that is the number to measure here. The more consequential part is `/swarm`. Google is shipping the orchestration layer — coordinator, parallel worktrees, adversarial verifiers, a shared board — as a platform feature, which is the harness [the 08-27 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/08/27/the-harness-and-the-price-of-a-task/) argued carries frontier agent performance. When the platform owns the coordinator, the agent framework gets commoditized and the quota becomes the budget line; Google's own warning is that parallel swarms burn quota much faster than chat.

## The model vendor shipped an office suite

**What happened.** Anthropic announced Claude Meetings, Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards, all in beta. Meetings joins a video call as a visible participant, follows the conversation, drafts the plan, builds a timeline on a whiteboard, answers in chat and pulls from connected apps; it launches on Team and Enterprise plans and works with Google Meet at launch, and leaves automatically if an external guest joins. Sheets generates a working spreadsheet with tabs, formulas, formatting and dropdowns and edits cells alongside the team; Whiteboards turns a brainstorm into a shared board, then a doc or slide deck. Both are beta on paid plans. Claude Data, formerly Dashboards, is generally available, and interactive connectors open Adobe, Atlassian and HubSpot apps inside Claude. Microsoft shares fell about 2% in morning trading.

**Why it matters.** The S-1 says 44% of nine-month revenue came through CSP marketplaces — someone else's storefront, billing and customer relationship, down from 48% a year earlier but still the channel. This morning Anthropic shipped its own: meetings, spreadsheets, whiteboards and connectors, sold into the paid seat, aimed at the workflow Microsoft owns. The model is the component; the seat is the distribution. For an operator, the interesting question is not whether Claude Sheets beats Excel — it won't, at first — but whether the connectors become the place where agents touch the systems of record. Adobe, Atlassian and HubSpot inside Claude is the shape of an agent that reads the ticket and writes the update; that is the workflow layer the model vendors have been circling for a year, and owning it is how a model vendor becomes an application vendor. The tell is the launch rail: Anthropic's meeting product starts inside Google Meet, not Teams — even the direct channel begins on someone else's platform. Microsoft's drop is the market pricing the seat, not the model.

## The Rest

- **AlphaProof Code formally verified Signal's protocol code in four weeks** — Gemini Carbon agents generated more than six million lines of Lean, flagged about 1% of the codebase for human review, and proved two reintroduced bugs insecure; the proof binds the implementation to a specification humans reviewed, so the spec remains the hard part, but a four-week clock changes what a security team can budget. [deepmind.google](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/)
- **Physical AI shipped edge-first** — NVIDIA's Alpamayo 2 Edge is a 2B-parameter VLA on DRIVE AGX Thor at 160–190 ms pixel-to-trajectory with open weights under OpenMDW 1.1, and DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 hit 96% pick-and-pack with Apptronik's Apollo 2 on Mercedes-Benz lines in Hungary and Germany; the line is the benchmark now, and the missing numbers are cycle time and cost per pick. [developer.nvidia.com](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/running-av-reasoning-models-within-onboard-compute-and-latency-limits/) · [deepmind.google](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-5-accelerates-real-world-physical-intelligence/)
- **NVIDIA open-sourced the agent runtime** — Agent Substrate, a CNCF Sandbox runtime, multiplexes sessions over warm Kubernetes pods, checkpointing idle sessions with memory and filesystem state at a claimed 10x container density; the claim to test is checkpoint/restore latency and tail latency at concurrency, because agents are bursty and idle, and the scheduler, not the model, is the cost center. [developer.nvidia.com](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/agent-substrate-advancing-kubernetes-for-agents-at-scale/)
- **Hugging Face put a classifier in front of the generator** — decision models return a probability distribution over fixed answers such as routing, urgency and refund without generating text; the example's usage line shows 148 input tokens and 0 output tokens, so the price is per decision, not per token, and the eval is per-decision accuracy. [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/blog/decision)
- **Bay launched with $50 million to check agent actions on the endpoint** — a static binary intercepts each proposed action with the session as context, returns a verdict in milliseconds or asks the user to approve, and blocks what it judges too risky; Micron, Wix, ClickUp and Peloton are customers, and the claimed false-positive rate is under 1% — the deterministic enforcement layer sold as a product. [siliconangle.com](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/10/ai-security-startup-bay-launches-with-50m-to-vet-agent-actions-before-they-run/)

## What I'm watching

The filed range still needs an effectiveness date, a roadshow price and a first trade — CNBC reports trading is expected around November 19 — and the lock-up is the number to watch, because the $26 billion placement is outside it. Gemini 4 Carbon's $4/$20 list lands December 1; the next three weeks are the only cheap window to reproduce Google's cost-per-task claim on your own workload. AlphaProof Code needs an independent reproduction and a cost per proof; Agent Substrate needs checkpoint/restore latency; Alpamayo 2 Edge needs a disengagement rate on a route Nvidia did not choose. And the Q3 compute ratio is the number to carry into the roadshow: 58% is what a delivered block does to a margin.
