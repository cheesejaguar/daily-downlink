Three stories this week describe the same shift. Anthropic committed $3.4 billion to take over a supplier's two-year-old order for 1.7 gigawatts of gas-fired generators — buying the queue, not just the power. Google donated its agent runtime, sandbox and RL loop to the CNCF and shipped Agent Substrate 1.0, turning the layer that runs agents into commodity plumbing. Apple trained a humanoid entirely in simulation and put its brain on a Mac mini strapped to the robot's back: 91% mean success across 360 trials in six homes it had never seen, and 70% on chained tasks. In each case the model is no longer where the constraint lives. The constraint has moved below it — into megawatts, schedulers and synthetic homes — and that is where the engineering bill is being paid.

## The power queue is the new GPU queue

**What happened.** The Financial Times reported over the weekend that Anthropic signed a $3.4 billion deal in late September with OnePWR Solutions, a San Antonio power supplier, for 1.7 gigawatts of large gas-fired generators. OnePWR had ordered the generators from Caterpillar about two years ago but needed money to complete the purchase; Anthropic took over the orders plus the work needed to install the machines. It did not buy OnePWR, and no staff moved. Neither company would comment, the locations are undisclosed, and Bloomberg's follow-up added no confirmation; the FT's account rests on one person familiar with the deal.

**Why it matters.** The shape of the deal is the tell. Anthropic did not commission new turbines and wait; it stepped into an order book that was already two years old. The deal is a bet that the generator lead time, not the GPU allocation, is now the longest pole: the scarce asset is the slot, and $3.4 billion is what the place in line costs. The company's amended S-1 lists $895.4 billion of commitments, $69.8 billion of them due inside twelve months; the power deal is the physical leg of a book that was, until now, mostly paper. [The 11-02 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/02/the-bill-arrived-itemized/) read that book as a schedule, not an efficiency claim; [the 09-18 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/09/18/physics-billed-the-frontier/) argued the binding constraint had moved from silicon to physics. This is what buying physics looks like: you pay for someone else's place in line. For an operator, the capacity plan's critical path now includes the generator order and the interconnect, not just the GPU allocation. The open question is the one the FT could not answer — where the 1.7 gigawatts land, and what they cost per megawatt-hour. Until those are named, the deal is a reservation, not a data center.

## The sandbox becomes commodity plumbing

**What happened.** On November 6, Google announced it had donated Agent Substrate, gVisor and OpenRL to the CNCF, after the projects were accepted into the CNCF Sandbox last month, and that Agent Substrate had reached 1.0 and is now generally available as Agent Substrate on GKE. The architecture separates stateful Actors — an agent's memory, files and identity — from a warm pool of Kubernetes Workers. When an agent pauses for a model turn, a tool call or a human, Agent Substrate snapshots its state to storage and returns the Worker to the pool; the next request restores the Actor onto any available Worker in under 500 milliseconds. Google claims 10x the density of standard container runtimes and more than 500 suspend-and-resume activations per second, with more than 100 contributors since development began in May 2026. gVisor isolates generated code inside a userspace application kernel; OpenRL runs self-hosted RL post-training loops in plain Python on Kubernetes. Early design partners include Nous Research, which is building Hermes Enterprise on it.

**Why it matters.** An agent spends most of its life waiting — on a model turn, a tool call or a human — and a live container per idle agent is a memory bill for nothing. Agent Substrate's answer is to make the agent's state portable and the compute pool shared, which turns cost per agent-hour into a scheduling problem. Google's 10x density is a vendor claim until an independent benchmark reproduces it, but the direction is not in doubt: the runtime layer is becoming neutral, open plumbing. The more consequential change is gVisor's move to CNCF. Sandboxed infrastructure providers, Google included, carry host-kernel patches that bring gVisor's I/O close to native; upstream Linux maintainers declined to merge them while one vendor owned the project. Neutral governance opens the path to upstreaming, which is the difference between a sandbox that is cheap to run anywhere and one that is cheap only where its owner has patched the kernel. [The 10-02 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/02/sandboxes-are-controls-not-a-containment-strategy/) made the distinction that survives the announcement: a sandbox is a control, not a containment strategy. Commodity plumbing is good for the control layer; it does not answer who owns the identity, what the policy is, or what happens when the agent goes wrong. [The 11-05 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/05/the-leash-is-the-product/) applied the operator test to a Kubernetes agent: can you print the effective permission list — tools, credential reach, writable roots — from the runtime? If yes, the commodity runtime is a cost win. If not, it is a nicer demo.

## Zero-shot sim-to-real is now a laptop problem

**What happened.** Apple's paper "Perceptive Humanoid Motion from Simulation" (arXiv 2611.06908) trained entirely in simulation: 162,698 episodes and 296.3 hours of training data at 30 Hz across 1,000 procedurally generated homes, using Infinigen scenes and Kimodo motion segments. The deployed robot is a stock Unitree G1 with two modifications: a head-mounted OAK-1W camera and a rear enclosure holding a Mac mini M4 Pro with 64 GB. The Mac mini runs the orchestrator — Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 4-bit in MLX, replanning roughly every two seconds — and the motion generator, a frozen Qwen3.5-2B VLM encoder with a DiT decoder that produces five-second whole-body plans (150 frames of 36 values) at about 3 Hz, committing the first half-second. A factory Jetson Orin NX runs a 50 Hz tracker for 29 joints. Across 360 real-world trials in six unmapped homes — no scene scan, no scripted trajectory, only the robot's egocentric camera — HALO averaged 91% success across six skills and 70% on chained two- and three-skill tasks.

**Why it matters.** The data engine is the product. A thousand procedural homes and 296 hours of synthetic motion replace the teleop dataset that usually gates a humanoid project, and the deployed hardware is a commodity G1 plus a Mac mini. The 91% is a real-robot number, not a simulation score, but the 70% on chained tasks is the production number: composition is the remaining gap, and so are hands — none of the six skills grasps. The six skills are authored, not discovered; a new task still needs a prompt and usually a keyframe, so this is a repertoire, not autonomous skill growth. [The 11-06 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/06/the-renewal-is-the-tell/) said a world model is a component in a loop — the policy still needs a simulator runtime, a scenario renderer and an eval. HALO is that loop closing on a laptop: the scenario generator is the moat, and the unseen-home trial is the eval that matters. [The 10-27 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/27/the-launch-number-is-not-the-production-number/) drew the line between a launch number and a production number; this paper draws it for robots.

## The Rest

- **METR's 1.9× acceleration finding came with its best evidence redacted.** The November 5 assessment of Anthropic's AI R&D, covering August 12 to October 29, puts AECI growth at 1.9× the pre-October-2025 rate, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5–2.8× — roughly two years of progress in one — and says AI in AI R&D likely drives most of it, with significant uncertainty. Anthropic redacted "a majority of the evidence we found most informative"; METR says the public must rely on its judgment "much more than would be ideal." An eval where the subject controls disclosure is attestation, not evidence, and [the 10-25 column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/25/the-caveats-live-in-the-artifact/) already made the interval the finding. [metr.org](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment)

- **OpenAI's Agents SDK alpha renames a security control and changes a write default.** v0.24.0-alpha.7, released November 8, makes new Environments default to `write_policy="strict"` — Linux or macOS executor, working directory writable, extra roots via `writable_roots` — renames MCP approval modes `auto` to `native` and `approve` to `preapproved` (the old values are rejected), and raises bundled reasoning effort from low to medium for `gpt-6-astra`, `gpt-6.1-sol` and `gpt-5.6-sol`. The approval string is a security control wearing a rename: pin the SDK, diff the policy, and test the upgrade before it touches a live agent. [github.com](https://github.com/openai/openai-agents-python/releases/tag/v0.24.0-alpha.7)

- **Anthropic's scientific-modeling post is about the ablation, not the fit.** A guest post from ELLIS Tübingen grantees retells Hodgkin-Huxley's 1951 model: the fourth-power term was an educated guess that fit the data, and decades later it turned out to match four identical channel subunits. The insight was not that the fourth power worked; it was that a simpler model failed. An AI that hands you the fitted model gives you an answer, not understanding — and an eval that reports a passing score without the ablation that shows what the model isn't doing is a number, not an insight. [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/teaching-ai-to-build-scientific-models)

- **The AI equity market is being asked how much more it can absorb.** The Information reports that tech giants, chip companies and AI cloud providers have sold or priced more than $300 billion of new stock this year, and asks how much more the market can take. [Yesterday's column](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/11/07/the-checkpoint-is-the-commodity/) recorded OpenAI's round closing below both its raise and valuation targets and Anthropic's IPO being repriced in public. The cost of capital is part of the cost of compute, and a market that absorbed $300 billion is not the same as one that will absorb the next tranche at the same price. [theinformation.com](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/ai-stock-can-market-absorb)

## What I'm watching

Anthropic's 1.7 gigawatts: where the generators land, who supplies the gas, and whether the $3.4 billion survives the IPO roadshow's diligence. Google's 10x density claim: the first independent Agent Substrate benchmark on a non-GKE cluster. Apple's HALO code: whether the six-skill repertoire reproduces on a different humanoid, and whether the next paper adds a hand. METR's redacted evidence: whether Anthropic publishes a fuller version, or an independent evaluator gets the same access. And OpenAI's SDK: the 0.24.0 stable release, where the alpha's write policy and approval strings become the default for everyone who upgrades.
