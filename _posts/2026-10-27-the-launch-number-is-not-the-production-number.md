Three stories in today's mix share one pattern: the number that gets quoted is never the number you can budget against. Genie 4 renders a coherent world for a short session, and Google's own limitations section says object permanence drifts as the session runs longer. NVIDIA's DGX Spark post leads with a 185% speculative-decoding gain on a synthetic workload; across all 1,319 GSM8K questions the same three optimizations gain 42.5%. GPT-6.1 Terra lists at $1/$5 per million tokens, and one max-reasoning image test emitted 22,629 output tokens for a bill of 11.3161 cents. In each case the number you can plan against is in the detail, not the headline.

## The verifier is what turns Genie 4 from a demo into a training environment

**What happened.** Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 on October 27 as a research preview for trusted testers, with access coming soon to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie and to early-access partners of the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. The world model is natively multimodal: it generates ambient sound, music and interactive dialogue alongside the scene, accepts real-time language steering (change the weather, move the camera, add a person), and simulates physics and human motion. The part that matters for training is the loop. Gemini 3.8 Flash acts as a guiding agent, observing the scene, breaking goals into a sequence of steps, reviewing incoming frames to verify that each step succeeded, and adapting the plan when the world or the user intervenes. DeepMind also ran Gemini 4 Argon against Genie 4 in a Gemini Robotics experiment, with Argon decomposing a user-instructed task into steps that Genie renders; the published example is Apptronik's Apollo humanoid playing checkers. All generated video and audio carry SynthID. The limitations section concedes that visual details and object permanence can shift or drift over longer sessions, after multiple complex prompt edits, or while navigating, and that legible text, spatial accuracy and long-term consistency remain unsolved. No weights, no model card and no API price are published.

**Why it matters.** Photorealism and audio are the demo; the verifier is the product. A world model becomes a training environment at the moment something can grade the rollout — otherwise it is a video generator with a keyboard. Gemini 3.8 Flash checking each frame against the intended step is the difference between "the render looks like a robot picking up a cup" and "the policy completed the task," and that is the only version of simulation that can tell a training run whether the task succeeded. The limitation is the tell for anyone budgeting it: object permanence drifts, so long-horizon tasks are exactly where the environment stops being faithful. A policy trained in a world that forgets where the mailbox is learns to expect a world that forgets the mailbox, or learns a shortcut that only works in the renderer. The post gives no drift-versus-session-length number, which is the one an operator would need before treating this as infrastructure rather than pre-visualization. DeepMind frames game use as prototype and concept design before writing engine code, which is pre-visualization, not a runtime. A game engine's job is deterministic state; Genie's job is plausible frames. Until the API arrives with a seed and determinism story and a price, this is a research preview with an extraordinary demo — [the world-model lane AMD bought into for $8.2 billion in September](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/09/28/amd-buys-world-labs/) is now two-sided, but the billing side is still blank.

## The 185% is a synthetic workload; the 42.5% is the number you can ship

**What happened.** NVIDIA's developer blog published a serving study of Ling-3.0-flash on a single DGX Spark: a 124-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts with 5.1 billion active parameters per token, 42 layers, 8 of 512 routed experts per token, and a native 256K-token context. The baseline was the official cookbook's W4A8 configuration. Three optimizations followed. Replacing the FlashInfer MXFP4 MoE backend with the Humming kernel library moved time per output token from 27.71 ms to 25.35 ms, a 9.3% throughput gain. Online FP8 quantization of the LM head halved its weight-read volume and, stacked on Humming, took decode throughput from 36.09 to 43.94 tokens per second, 21.7% over baseline. Multi-token prediction with the model's built-in NEXTN layer accepted 3.90 tokens per round on a synthetic workload and raised throughput 185.1%. On the full GSM8K test set, all three together produced decode throughput of 37.81 to 53.89 tokens per second, a 42.5% gain, mean latency of 15.21 to 11.49 seconds, a 24.5% reduction, and accuracy of 96.89% to 96.82% — a difference the post calls not statistically detectable. The accepted length on that real workload was 2.68 tokens per round, not 3.90.

**Why it matters.** The gap between 185% and 42.5% is the engineering, not a marketing lie. Speculative decoding's gain scales with how often the draft is accepted, and acceptance is workload-dependent: 3.90 tokens per round on a synthetic stream, 2.68 on GSM8K. A vendor headline measured on a synthetic stream is a ceiling; the test set is the number you can plan against. The more valuable paragraph is the stacking trap. All three optimizations require the draft model to share the full LM-head module, and if it does not, the post says the system "silently degrades to a configuration slower than the unoptimized baseline." The mechanism is specific: the draft receives FP8 weights but interprets them as BF16, accept length collapses to 1.00, and throughput falls below a configuration with no optimizations. That is a failure mode that looks enabled — the flags are set, the model loads, the throughput is just worse. The operator's rule is to assert each optimization at startup rather than infer configuration from performance, because a slow number can mean a busy GPU or a broken stack, and after the fact you cannot tell them apart. [The 10-20 column measured a two-Spark cluster at 2.9 bits a weight and found the better model 3.6 times slower per task at max effort](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/20/v41-flash-1017-on-two-sparks/); the lesson there was that the rate card is not the bill. This is the vendor's own version: the speedup is not the speed.

## Terra is a routing tier, and the bill is the thinking time

**What happened.** OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra, shipped yesterday at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output, is positioned by the company on visual reasoning and computer use. In a Hacker News comment on October 26, OpenAI's Ted Sanders gave the candid version: Terra is "probably better than Astra and Sol at technical communication, similar on vision and computer use (but much faster!), and a bit worse on most other things" — "a nice midrange option" for developers who want something cheaper than Sol but smarter than Luna, and "not a bombshell release" for subscribers. Simon Willison's max-reasoning pelican test cost 11.3161 cents and took 4 minutes 11 seconds; the billing detail behind that number is 22,629 output tokens against 16 input tokens, which at $5 and $1 per million is exactly 11.3161 cents. The model lists a 1,050,000-token context.

**Why it matters.** At the cheap tier, almost the entire bill is output. One image and a short prompt produced 22,629 output tokens because max reasoning spends its budget thinking, and at $5 per million the output alone is 11.31 cents; the input rate is a rounding error. The operator's arithmetic is cost per completed task, not cost per token, and the variable you control is reasoning length, not the rate card. That is also why Terra is a routing decision rather than a replacement. OpenAI's Ted Sanders says it is better at technical communication, similar on vision and computer use but much faster, and worse at most other things. Put it on the repetitive, high-volume, visual and computer-use work where speed matters; keep Sol or Opus for the calls where being wrong is expensive. A midrange model that is honest about being midrange is more useful than a launch deck that is not. [Yesterday's column put Terra's price reset in The Rest](https://blog.aaronx.co/2026/10/26/anthropics-s-1-says-the-agent-economy-is-a-business/); the independent read today says the price is real and the tier is the story, not the benchmark crown.
