Cohere announced this morning the initial close of a Series E targeting up to C$7 billion at a C$35.7 billion valuation — the largest private capital raise in Canadian history. The companies of Schwarz Group led the round; the Government of Canada is putting up to C$1.6 billion in for an equity stake, alongside new investors Bell Ventures and Bullhound Capital and returning backers Nvidia, Inovia and Prosperity7. The terms include a C$1.7 billion commitment to build out Canadian compute infrastructure by 2030 and a promise to remain headquartered in Canada — "Cohere will stay Canadian," Prime Minister Mark Carney said at the announcement. Cohere says it ended Q3 with 165% year-over-year ARR growth and 300 customers, up 20x in a year. This is the sovereign-AI pitch with a sovereign balance sheet behind it.

## The sovereign alternative now has a home government

**What happened.** Cohere announced the initial close of its Series E, targeting up to C$7 billion at a C$35.7 billion valuation; the round is oversubscribed and the company says it will close soon. The companies of Schwarz Group are the lead investor; new investors include Bell Ventures, Bullhound Capital and the Government of Canada; Inovia Capital, Nvidia and Prosperity7 Ventures are returning. The Globe and Mail reports Ottawa is putting C$1.6 billion into the deal for an equity stake, and that Cohere committed to spend C$1.7 billion on Canadian compute infrastructure by 2030 and to remain headquartered in Canada. Cohere reports 165% year-over-year ARR growth in Q3 2026 and a customer base that grew 20x to 300.

**Why it matters.** For an operator, the number that matters is not the valuation but the C$1.7 billion compute commitment: sovereign AI has been sold as a deployment mode — on-prem, air-gapped, data residency — and this round turns it into an industrial policy with a cap table. A government equity stake is a procurement anchor for Canadian public-sector buyers and a credible answer to "who stands behind this vendor"; it also makes the "neutral alternative to the US and China" a national champion with a home team. If you are buying sovereign AI for regulatory defensibility, the questions are now whether Ottawa's stake comes with procurement commitments, what the compute-localization clause does to your residency terms, and whether the neutrality you were buying survives the shareholder list. The round funds compute and distribution, not just R&D; the next tell is whether a C$1.6 billion check becomes a standard rather than a one-off.
