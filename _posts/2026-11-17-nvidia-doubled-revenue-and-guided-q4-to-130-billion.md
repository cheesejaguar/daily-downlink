NVIDIA reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion tonight, up 103% from a year ago and about $6.7 billion above the $109.1 billion Street consensus, with data center at $107.6 billion, up 110%. It guided the fourth quarter to $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, against a $125.1 billion consensus, and said Vera Rubin NVL72 is shipping at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Azure, Oracle and Nebius in what it calls the fastest product ramp in its history. Gross margin was 74.9% non-GAAP; the Q4 guide steps down to 71–72%. This is the first print under interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress, and it answers the demand question the 24% November selloff was asking. It does not answer the succession question.

## The guide is the number; the margin is the tell

**What happened.** Revenue $115.8 billion, up 20% sequentially and 103% year over year; data center $107.6 billion, up 110%; GAAP EPS $2.70, non-GAAP $2.68 against a $2.48 adjusted consensus; net income $65.3 billion. The Q4 guide of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, includes an extra week and assumes no China data center compute revenue. Gross margin is guided to 71–72%, down from 74.9%. NVIDIA added $150 billion to its buyback authorization in September, returned $28.1 billion in the quarter, and has $227.3 billion remaining.

**Why it matters.** Demand is no longer the open question, but the guide is doing two jobs at once. The extra week inflates the sequential step, and the margin guide gives back three to four points — the bottom NVIDIA flagged in August as memory prices rose. For an operator, that is the input cost behind every GPU-hour and reserved-capacity bill in 2027. The planning number is now $130 billion; the tell is whether the call quantifies the memory drag and whether the board names a successor before the Q4 print. A guide delivered by an acting CEO who is also the CFO is a plan with a governance discount — the risk premium on 2027 allocations.
